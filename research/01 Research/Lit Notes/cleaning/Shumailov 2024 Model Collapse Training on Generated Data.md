# The Curse of Recursion: Model Collapse (Shumailov, 2024)

**Basis:** arXiv 2305.17493v3 (14 April 2024) read via alphaXiv PDF query on 2026-11-08.

**Takeaway:** Models trained on data generated by earlier models lose the tails of the true distribution first and then converge towards a low-variance point; this happens for Gaussian mixtures, VAEs and a fine-tuned language model, and keeping some original data slows but does not stop it.

## Problem
What happens when generated data feeds the training of later models?

## Data and period
Synthetic Gaussian and GMM data, MNIST-style VAE, OPT-125m fine-tuned on wikitext2 (§5).

## Method
Theory for discrete and Gaussian cases (statistical and functional approximation error) plus recursive-training experiments.

## Baselines and validation
Model trained on real data (generation 0).

## Results (with page or section references)
- Early collapse loses tail information; late collapse converges to a low-variance distribution (Definition 3.1, p. 3).
- VAE latents: "tails get washed away" over generations (p. 10).
- OPT-125m: perplexity rises over generations; preserving 10% of original data gives only minor degradation (pp. 11–12).

## Limits and risks to validity
Not financial; recursive multi-generation setting, stronger than one round of imputation. The relevant lesson is mechanism: generators under-sample rare events, which are what shock detection needs.
