# Frontiers of Real-Time Data Analysis (Croushore, 2011)

**Basis:** author's December 2009 manuscript (opened 2026-11-08; first ~100,000 of 104,618 characters); published version JEL 2011 (EconPapers record seen). Numbers not checked against the journal version.

**Takeaway:** Macro data revisions are large (2008Q4 real GDP growth revised from −3.8% to −6.2%; mean absolute initial-to-latest output revision 1.67%) and can reverse forecasting conclusions, so forecasts must be built and judged on real-time vintages.

## Problem
How much data revisions matter for forecasting and research.

## Data and period
Review of real-time research; RTDSM (online since 1999), ALFRED, FRASER.

## Method
Survey across data revisions, forecasting, monetary policy, macro research, current analysis.

## Baselines and validation
Reviewed studies compare first-release, real-time and latest-available data.

## Results (with page or section references)
- Largest recorded quarterly real GDP revision, 2008Q4 (p. 3).
- Revisions average −0.08 pp in recessions, +0.18 pp in expansions (p. 3).
- Output mean absolute revision initial→latest 1.67% (Table 3, p. 12).
- Evaluating against latest-available data "may provide a distorted view of forecast ability" (p. 31).
- Leading-index and money-output results weaken or reverse in real time (pp. 8, 26–27); exchange-rate predictability appears only with real-time data (p. 32).
- "If you want to analyze policy or forecasts, you must use real-time data" (p. 42).

## Limits and risks to validity
Macro-focused, pre-2010. Manuscript version. A survey, so its rubric score reflects the evidence it collects rather than one test.
