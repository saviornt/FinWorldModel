# Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 3: Generative AI Training (pre-publication) (U.S. Copyright Office, 2025)

**Basis:** official documentation opened 2026-11-08.

**Takeaway:** The US Copyright Office found that collecting and training on copyrighted works implicates the reproduction right, that fair use is fact-specific, and that training for research, analysis or non-substitutive tasks is more likely transformative and fair than training to produce competing content; the report is not binding.

## Problem
Whether training a model on copyrighted text (news, posts, papers) is lawful in the US absent a licence.

## Data and period
Not applicable.

## Method
Report pages 1-33 opened directly (the fetch tool truncated before the fair-use conclusion); the conclusions were read in a law-firm summary (Lowenstein Sandler client alert, 2025-05-12, https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/us-copyright-office-releases-long-awaited-report-on-generative-ai-training-and-copyright-law), opened 2026-11-08.

## Baselines and validation
Not applicable.

## Results (with page or section references)
- Data collection and curation "implicates the reproduction right" (p. 27); fair use is "the primary defense available" (p. 32).
- Memorisation may be relevant to fair use (n. 178, p. 31).
- Per the Lowenstein summary: training on large diverse data can often be transformative, especially for research, analysis or non-substitutive tasks; factual works weigh toward fair use; market harm is the most important factor; noncommercial research that does not enable reproduction is more likely fair; publicly available is "not synonymous with 'authorized.'"
- Recommends letting voluntary licensing develop without immediate government intervention.

## Limits and risks to validity
Pre-publication version; not binding; issued amid leadership changes. Copyright is only one layer: contract terms (vendor licences) can forbid training even where copyright would allow it. US-only; EU text-and-data-mining rules not read. Not legal advice.
