# Assessing Look-Ahead Bias in GPT Sentiment (Glasserman, 2023)

**Basis:** v1, page excerpts via alphaXiv (pp. 1–3, 5, 7–10, 12–13, 15, 17).

**Takeaway:** Anonymising company names in headlines made GPT-3.5 sentiment strategies do better in sample, so the "distraction" from company knowledge outweighed look-ahead gains; out of sample the difference was not significant except borderline for large firms.

## Problem
How much do look-ahead bias and general company knowledge distort LLM-sentiment backtests inside the training window?

## Data and period
Scraped headlines and Thomson Reuters S&P 500 headlines; in-sample within GPT-3.5's training window and out-of-sample from Oct 2021 (~314 trading days) (pp. 2, 9).

## Method
GPT-3.5 good/bad/neutral scores on original vs anonymised headlines (fuzzy matching and Knowledge Graph replacement); equal-weighted daily long–short portfolios; stacked regressions with Wald tests (pp. 5, 12–13).

## Baselines and validation
Original vs anonymised headlines only; in-sample vs out-of-sample comparison. Strategies are explicitly not intended to be feasible: no costs (p. 5).

## Results (with page or section references)
- In sample, anonymised beats original by 5.89 bp/day (scraped, p = 0.066) and 4.37 bp/day (TR, p = 0.017) (Table 3, p. 9).
- Out of sample: scraped difference not significant (p = 0.23); TR anonymised better, borderline (p = 0.064) (Table 3).
- Effect concentrated in larger firms (pp. 3, 12).
- Overconfidence: original-wrong / anonymised-neutral cases lose 242 bp on average (p. 10).

## Limits and risks to validity
_My assessment:_ one model (GPT-3.5), short out-of-sample window, no costs, simplified strategy. Masking was later shown to be leaky ([[Lopez-Lira 2025 LLM Memorization Problem]]; [[Dehkordi 2026 OLIVIA LLM News Look-Ahead Test]]), so "anonymised" may not mean unidentified.
