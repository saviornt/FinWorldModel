# Six Dimensions of Benchmarking Time-Series Databases (SciTSv2) (Jalal Mostafa et al., 2026)

**Takeaway:** Single-axis benchmarks hide bottlenecks: varying parallelism, batch size, regularity, series width and mixed read/write load showed TimescaleDB's Postgres backend stalling beyond moderate parallelism with heavy disk amplification, InfluxDB query latency growing steeply with many variables, and read/write contention as the dominant limit.

## Problem
Which storage engines stay efficient under realistic, mixed time-series workloads.

## Data and period
Synthetic scientific/IoT-style workloads generated by the SciTSv2 framework (open source on GitHub), swept over six dimensions.

## Method
Benchmark of InfluxDB (TSM tree), TimescaleDB (relational, tuned per vendor advice), ClickHouse (columnar) and DataLayerTS (regular-series) with fine-grained CPU, I/O and disk metrics.

## Baselines and validation
Four engines of distinct design compared against each other on identical hardware; no embedded/file baseline (DuckDB/Parquet).

## Results (with page or section references)
- TimescaleDB saturates early; its PostgreSQL backend stalls beyond moderate parallelism; extreme disk amplification with wide series (sec. 7).
- ClickHouse performs worse on regular data (sparse-index parts concentrate writes) (sec. 7).
- InfluxDB query latency grows exponentially with many variables per point; tag indexing unsuited to high-dimensional lookups (sec. 7).
- Concurrent ingestion plus queries: read/write contention is the dominant limitation (sec. 7).

## Limits and risks to validity
Scientific/IoT workloads, not market data; no QuestDB, kdb+, DuckDB or ArcticDB; no statistical tests across runs reported in the parts read.
