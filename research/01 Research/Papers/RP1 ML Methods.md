# RP1 – ML Methods for Multimodal Financial Forecasting and Shock Detection

## Abstract

Across 163 sources (14 Strong, 85 Moderate, 64 Weak), strong simple baselines are rarely beaten. For returns, a random walk and gradient-boosted trees are hard to beat, generic time-series foundation models seldom do; for volatility, HAR remains the benchmark, with small gains from ML and regime information (RQ1.1). Pre-cutoff LLM backtests are invalid because of memorisation, and no study found shows an LLM backbone helping numeric forecasting (RQ1.2). Dictionary sentiment is weakest, benchmark accuracy does not rank tools by return value, and daily sentiment decays within one to two days (RQ1.3). Linked-firm returns predict returns, and shocks spread along supply chains, but GNNs show no clear gain over neighbour features (RQ1.4). Text novelty forecasts volatility and returns at monthly horizons; event-level alarms and price-only warnings are unreliable, and no source predicts shock timing (RQ1.5, RQ1.8). Volatility-filtered fat-tailed models are the tail baseline, and no method read stays calibrated at shock onset (RQ1.6). Fusion and added world state help only through gated, compressed pathways; naive concatenation hurts (RQ1.7, RQ1.15). Deep RL has no robust edge after costs; light adaptation matches meta-learning; no A* trading study was found (RQ1.9). A categorical output over normalised return buckets is a sound target, but token models are not shown to beat continuous ones or strong baselines, and sampled paths are poorly calibrated (RQ1.10, RQ1.13). Scalar sentiment adds little to strong price models, while return-supervised encoders give cross-sectional signal (RQ1.14). Markets over-react to recombined old news and then reverse (in-sample); bots, fakes and campaigns distort volume and small caps (RQ1.11). Liquid scheduled news is priced in milliseconds to seconds; slower diffusion remains across linked, small and foreign markets (RQ1.12).

## Research questions

- **RQ1.1** – Which price and volume models beat a random walk, GARCH and gradient-boosted trees out of sample, for returns versus volatility? Include statistical, deep, general foundation and finance-specific foundation models
- **RQ1.2** – How well do LLMs work as (a) readers of financial text and (b) predictors of price tokens? How large is the look-ahead and memorisation problem?
- **RQ1.3** – How do lexicon, FinBERT-style and LLM sentiment compare? How fast does the signal decay? Do news and social posts differ?
- **RQ1.4** – Do company graphs (supply chain, sector, co-movement, co-mention) improve forecasts? Do GNNs beat simple neighbour features? Do shocks spread along supply chains?
- **RQ1.5** – Can bursts or novelty in news, social posts or papers flag market shocks early? What lead times and false-alarm rates are reported?
- **RQ1.6** – Which tail and uncertainty methods (quantile models, conformal prediction, extreme value theory, deep VaR) stay calibrated in crises?
- **RQ1.7** – Does multimodal fusion beat the best single modality? Compare late, early, cross-attention and mixture-of-experts fusion, and note when each helps
- **RQ1.8** – What does the literature say about predicting versus detecting rare, unprecedented events? Where is the theoretical limit?
- **RQ1.9** – Is there evidence for reinforcement learning, MAML-inspired meta-learning (first-order MAML, Reptile, meta-learned adapters, in-context adaptation) or search-based planning (such as A\*) in forecasting or trading decisions?
- **RQ1.10** – How should numerical market data be represented for sequence models: fixed bins, learned codebooks (vector quantisation), hybrid tokenizers, or continuous features fed directly? What does discretisation lose, and do token models beat continuous-input models?
- **RQ1.11** – How can text and event streams tell independent corroboration from repetition of one report? How are bots, coordinated campaigns, misinformation and source reliability detected, and does filtering them improve market signals?
- **RQ1.12** – How fast does information move from first observation to wide dissemination to price? How do studies measure what is already priced in (options, overseas markets, pre-announcement drift)?
- **RQ1.13** – Do autoregressive next-token models trained on market data (no LLM inside, e.g. Kronos, Chronos-style tokenized forecasters, decoder-only time-series models) beat continuous-input models and simple baselines? Do they improve with more data and model size, how fast do errors compound over multi-step rollouts, and how well do sampled token paths give calibrated probabilistic and tail forecasts?
- **RQ1.14** – In what form should news, social posts and events enter a non-LLM autoregressive market model: sentiment or event tokens from a small encoder (such as FinBERT), structured event codes (such as GDELT/CAMEO), learned text embeddings, or text tokens interleaved with market tokens? Which forms carry signal, at what cost, and which avoid look-ahead and memorisation?
- **RQ1.15** – World-model hypothesis: does conditioning a market sequence model on a broader state of the world (macro, cross-asset, company graph, events) make forecasts more accurate than a price-only model, for which targets and horizons, and when does the added state hurt? What do financial and general world-model studies report?

## Findings

### RQ1.1 – Which price and volume models beat a random walk, GARCH and gradient-boosted trees out of sample, for returns versus volatility? Include statistical, deep, general foundation and finance-specific foundation models

**Status:** answered 2026-11-07 (12 sources: 11 empirical, 1 review).

**Answer.** *Returns:* a zero-return random walk is very hard to beat, and the best models beat it only by a small margin. On monthly US stocks the best models reach an R²_oos of about 0.3–0.4%, with trees and shallow neural nets ahead of linear models but not significantly different from each other, and no gain from depth beyond three layers ([[Gu 2020 Empirical Asset Pricing Machine Learning]]). On daily excess returns, tree ensembles (CatBoost) are the strongest benchmark: off-the-shelf time-series foundation models (TSFMs) lose to them, fine-tuning barely helps, and pretraining a TSFM from scratch on financial data narrows but does not close the gap ([[Rahimikia 2025 ReVisiting TSFMs in Finance]]). Zero-shot TSFMs on single large-cap stocks top the rankings against deep nets trained from scratch, but beat the random walk significantly in only 2 of 10 cases ([[Noguer i Alonso 2026 Pretrained TSFMs Return Forecasting]]). Intraday, the studies found either no signal ([[Mesfin 2026 LSTM vs Gradient Boosting MNQ]]) or a frozen TSFM no better than the historical mean ([[Dewage 2026 Hybrid Correction Frozen TSFM]]). Predictability depends on the regime: it appears in low-volatility states and vanishes in high-volatility states ([[Fang 2026 Volatility Regimes Return Prediction China]]). Economically, large gross Sharpe ratios from daily rebalancing turn negative for every model at 20 bps ([[Rahimikia 2025 ReVisiting TSFMs in Finance]]).

*Volatility:* the HAR family remains the benchmark to beat. Random forests and neural nets beat HAR for realised variance, with larger gains at weekly and monthly horizons and with extra predictors such as implied volatility ([[Christensen 2023 Machine Learning Volatility Forecasting]]). Zero-shot TSFMs mostly do not beat Log-HAR: only one small model (TTM) does, by about 1.5%, and an equal-weight TTM + Log-HAR average is the most robust choice ([[Brini 2026 TSFMs vs Econometric Realized Volatility]]). Kronos reports beating GARCH on realised-volatility MAE ([[Shi 2025 Kronos Financial Foundation Model]]), but GARCH is a weaker baseline than HAR, and HAR-RV beats frozen Kronos at longer horizons in a separate study ([[Nguyen 2026 Volatility-Clustering Adaptation]]). A regime probability added to HARQ helps most in stress periods ([[Fang 2026 Volatility Regimes Return Prediction China]]).

*Finance-specific foundation models:* the evidence agrees that domain matters. Generic TSFMs contain under ~1% financial data ([[Shi 2025 Kronos Financial Foundation Model]], Table 3), and models pretrained on financial data beat generic ones ([[Rahimikia 2025 ReVisiting TSFMs in Finance]]; [[Shi 2025 Kronos Financial Foundation Model]]). Only the Kronos paper itself claims a finance FM beats all baselines, and it uses one test window, no significance tests, and no random-walk or GBT baseline for returns. Naive fine-tuning of Kronos can make it worse than the frozen model ([[Nguyen 2026 Volatility-Clustering Adaptation]]). In a Sharpe-optimised multi-asset futures setting, gated recurrent and hybrid models beat generic Transformers and state-space models, which did no better than an AR(1) benchmark; the ranking held net of 0–5 bp costs ([[Saly-Kaufmann 2026 Deep Learning Financial Benchmark]]).

**Confidence:** High that a random walk (returns) and HAR (volatility) are hard benchmarks, that tree ensembles are the strongest cheap baseline for returns, and that generic zero-shot TSFMs rarely beat them. Medium on the size of gains from finance-native foundation models and on ML gains over HAR beyond the DJIA sample.

**Where sources disagree.** Kronos reports large gains for a finance FM over all baselines; the Strong-rated studies (Rahimikia; Brini) find generic TSFMs weak and finance-native ones still behind GBT or HAR. **What would change this answer:** an independent, multi-window test of Kronos (or another finance FM) against a random walk, CatBoost and HAR with DM/MCS tests and net-of-cost results; finance-native TSFM results at weekly or monthly rebalancing after costs; HAR vs ML comparisons on a broader universe that includes delisted stocks.

### RQ1.2 – How well do LLMs work as (a) readers of financial text and (b) predictors of price tokens? How large is the look-ahead and memorisation problem?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-07 (10 sources: 2 Strong, 6 Moderate, 2 Weak).

**Answer.** *(a) LLMs as readers of financial text.* Large LLMs read the direction of firm news well, but most of what they read is already in the price before a trader can act. On headlines published after GPT-4's training cutoff, GPT-4 calls the direction of the initial reaction right on ~90% of days. That move is not tradable. The 1–2 day drift after it gives a gross long–short Sharpe of 2.97, which falls to −0.39 at 20 bps round trip, to 0.56 when value-weighted, and from 6.54 (2021Q4) to 1.22 (early 2024) ([[Lopez-Lira 2026 ChatGPT Forecast Stock Prices]]). The strongest test so far was pre-registered and blinded on the point-in-time S&P 500 for 2026. In it, model readings line up with the overnight move to the open (IC 0.074–0.077) but not with the move after the open; a validated signal fell from IC 0.065 to 0.020, and no strategy survived costs and a deflated Sharpe ratio ([[Dehkordi 2026 OLIVIA LLM News Look-Ahead Test]]). Point-in-time encoders show that removing hindsight is affordable: NoLBERT topics forecast about as well as LDA ([[Foley 2026 NoLBERT News Topics Returns]]), and time-locked DatedGPT still earns a gross Sharpe of 3.20 on headlines ([[Yan 2026 DatedGPT Time-Aware Pretraining]]). Text signals still did not beat a price-only benchmark net of costs in the one study that tested this ([[Foley 2026 NoLBERT News Topics Returns]]). A live, web-searching agent picked top winners in the Russell 1000 for nine months (daily alpha 18.4 bp) but could not pick losers ([[Chen 2026 Agentic AI Nowcasting Returns]]). Model size matters for prompt-based reading: FinBERT and BERT prompted the same way give negative drift Sharpe ratios ([[Lopez-Lira 2026 ChatGPT Forecast Stock Prices]]; see RQ1.3). Claims that LLM-extracted event structures beat FinBERT rest on pre-cutoff news without memorisation controls ([[Li 2025 Structured Event Representation Returns]]).

*(b) LLMs as predictors of price tokens.* No evidence that a language-model backbone helps numeric forecasting. Removing the LLM from Time-LLM, CALF and OneFitsAll gives equal or better accuracy (including on exchange rates) at up to 1,000× less compute ([[Tan 2024 Are LMs Useful for Time Series]]). On a price-only agent benchmark, simple momentum extrapolation beat seven of nine frontier LLMs, and frontier scores fell by 40–64% from the earlier to the later test window while momentum held steady. That pattern fits memorisation of the earlier window, though the paper does not test it. Outcome-based post-training of a 4B model mainly improved magnitude, not direction ([[Guo 2026 Language Models Forecast Stock Prices]]). Finance-specific token models trained on prices (Kronos) are covered in RQ1.1.

*(c) Size of the look-ahead and memorisation problem.* Large for numbers, smaller and model-dependent for sentiment. Inside its training window GPT-4o recalls S&P 500 levels (MAPE 0.61%, direction 80.6%) and collapses after the cutoff (16.9%, 45.7%). Telling it to respect a fake cutoff fails (98% accuracy after the fake cutoff vs 40% on truly unseen data), and pre-cutoff forecasting skill is not identified ([[Lopez-Lira 2025 LLM Memorization Problem]]). Masking does not anonymise: commercial models pick masked companies out of four in 62–74% of cases against a 28% baseline ([[Dehkordi 2026 OLIVIA LLM News Look-Ahead Test]]). For headline-sentiment returns, a vintage that has seen the outcome period raises the effect from 28.3 to 33.0 bp per SD, and its extra signal is highly significant (t = 10.65) ([[Yan 2026 DatedGPT Time-Aware Pretraining]]). For GPT-3.5 sentiment, company knowledge hurt more than look-ahead helped: anonymised headlines did better in sample ([[Glasserman 2023 Look-Ahead Bias GPT Sentiment]]).

**Confidence:** High that pre-cutoff LLM backtests are not valid evidence (memorisation is large, prompts and masking do not fix it) and that LLM backbones add nothing to numeric time-series forecasting. Medium that LLM text reading has tradable value: gross predictability after the cutoff is well documented, but the one blinded Strong test is negative, the edge sits at the open and in small caps, and it has decayed.

**Where sources disagree.** The post-cutoff studies (Lopez-Lira & Tang; DatedGPT; Chen & Pu) report high gross Sharpe ratios, while the blinded test (Dehkordi et al.) finds nothing tradable after the open. The two can both hold: the early studies measure close-to-close or open-to-close returns in all US stocks, or gross returns, while the blinded test splits the move at the open in large caps and applies costs and multiple-testing corrections. The size of the look-ahead effect also differs by task: dominant for numeric recall ([[Lopez-Lira 2025 LLM Memorization Problem]]), significant but modest for headline sentiment ([[Yan 2026 DatedGPT Time-Aware Pretraining]]), negative net of the distraction effect for GPT-3.5 ([[Glasserman 2023 Look-Ahead Bias GPT Sentiment]]).

**What would change this answer:** the pre-registered forward test in [[Dehkordi 2026 OLIVIA LLM News Look-Ahead Test]] (news after Oct 2026); a longer live record from [[Chen 2026 Agentic AI Nowcasting Returns]] across a down market; a large point-in-time LLM (e.g. Kelly et al. 2026, not opened) beating a price-only GBT net of costs; any study showing an LLM backbone helps numeric return forecasting against a random walk and GBT.

### RQ1.3 – How do lexicon, FinBERT-style and LLM sentiment compare? How fast does the signal decay? Do news and social posts differ?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-08 (11 sources: 9 new — 1 Strong, 2 Moderate, 6 Weak — plus 2 RQ1.2 notes that also bear on it).

**Answer.** *(a) Lexicon vs FinBERT vs LLM.* Two rankings must be kept apart. On human-labelled text the order is clear: dictionaries reach 54–65% on Financial PhraseBank, finance transformers over 90%, zero-shot open LLMs about 92% and Claude Opus 4.5 93.6% ([[Bisharat 2026 Evaluating Financial Sentiment AI]]); QLoRA-tuned 7–8B LLMs reach macro-F1 0.86–0.88 against 0.68 for off-the-shelf FinBERT ([[Luo 2026 QLoRA Sentiment Return Predictability]]); on X posts an LLM agrees with a human coder far better than FinBERT (κ 0.74 vs 0.08) ([[Aravinthakshan 2026 Human Agreement vs Return Association]]). That order does not carry over to returns. Dictionaries are the weakest return signal wherever they are tested: the Loughran–McDonald score is insignificant for next-day returns ([[Kirtac 2024 Sentiment Trading with LLMs]]), its lead effects fail FDR on X posts ([[Aravinthakshan 2026 Human Agreement vs Return Association]]), and VADER carries nothing on r/WallStreetBets ([[Kilian 2026 LLM vs Lexicon Meme Stocks]]). Between FinBERT and LLMs the evidence splits. Large prompted GPT-class models beat FinBERT on post-cutoff headlines (drift Sharpe 1.66 for GPT-3.5 vs −0.33 for FinBERT; [[Lopez-Lira 2026 ChatGPT Forecast Stock Prices]]), and fine-tuned OPT beat FinBERT in a study with a random, leaky split ([[Kirtac 2024 Sentiment Trading with LLMs]]). But in the three studies that score labels and returns through one pipeline, the more accurate model was not the better predictor: FinBERT had the best one-day rank IC on 2019 headlines (0.0143 vs 0.0083 for QLoRA Qwen), and none of 28 tests survived FDR ([[Luo 2026 QLoRA Sentiment Return Predictability]]); none of twelve tools, nor Claude, predicted next-day returns after earnings releases, though several tracked the earnings surprise ([[Bisharat 2026 Evaluating Financial Sentiment AI]]); and FinBERT had the largest one-day lead on X posts despite near-chance label agreement ([[Aravinthakshan 2026 Human Agreement vs Return Association]]).

*(b) Decay.* A daily signal is mostly gone within one to two days. News tone adds 0.17% on day 1 and 0.04% on day 2, then nothing ([[Heston 2017 News vs Sentiment Returns]]); LLM headline drift lasts one to two days ([[Lopez-Lira 2026 ChatGPT Forecast Stock Prices]]); classifier ICs are positive on day 1 and negative from day 2 ([[Luo 2026 QLoRA Sentiment Return Predictability]]); on X posts the same-day link is strongest and the two-day lead is near zero ([[Aravinthakshan 2026 Human Agreement vs Return Association]]). Aggregation changes this: tone summed over a week predicts returns for up to 13 weeks, driven by negative news and by the next earnings announcement ([[Heston 2017 News vs Sentiment Returns]]). Cross-platform social sentiment also persists to t+20 without reversal, while social attention partly reverses ([[Cookson 2024 The Social Signal]]). Signals also decay across calendar time: LLM news Sharpe fell from 2021 to 2024 (RQ1.2), and social sentiment lost most of its next-day power after the January 2021 GameStop episode ([[Cookson 2024 The Social Signal]]).

*(c) News vs social posts.* They behave differently. Heavy news coverage is followed by lower volatility and turnover, heavy social coverage by higher volatility and turnover, consistent with social networks echoing stale news ([[Jiao 2020 Social Media News Media Stock Market]]). For returns, the common social sentiment factor predicts about +6 bp per SD next day (news sentiment about +8 bp), while social attention predicts about −13 bp and partly reverses ([[Cookson 2024 The Social Signal]]). On r/WallStreetBets, attention and volume carry the signal and sentiment does not ([[Cookson 2024 The Social Signal]]; [[Kmak 2025 ChatGPT Reddit Sentiment Prices]]; [[Kilian 2026 LLM vs Lexicon Meme Stocks]]). Social streams are polluted: 17.6% of the X corpus was spam, and posts from news accounts carried the strongest signal ([[Aravinthakshan 2026 Human Agreement vs Return Association]]).

**Confidence:** High that dictionaries give the weakest return signal and that benchmark accuracy does not rank tools by return usefulness (several independent tests, consistent direction). High that daily sentiment signals decay within one to two days. Medium on news versus social differences (one Strong study; the volatility evidence is in-sample). Low on whether LLM sentiment beats FinBERT for returns net of costs: the pro-LLM results are either leaky or rely on very large models, and the 7–8B and zero-shot tests are underpowered and capped for pre-cutoff use.

Holding those fixed, no study read shows an LLM beating FinBERT for returns.

### RQ1.4 – Do company graphs (supply chain, sector, co-movement, co-mention) improve forecasts? Do GNNs beat simple neighbour features? Do shocks spread along supply chains?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-07 (13 sources: 0 Strong, 9 Moderate, 4 Weak; lit notes in `01 Research/Lit Notes/graph/`).

**Answer.** *(a) Do company graphs help?* Linked-firm information predicts returns, and this is one of the better-established findings in empirical finance. Last month's returns of a firm's major customers predicted its next-month return by about 1.55% a month long–short over 1980–2004 ([[Cohen 2008 Economic Links Predictable Returns]]). A neighbour average over the shared-analyst-coverage graph did better still (monthly alpha 1.68%, t 9.67, 1984–2015) and absorbed industry, geographic, customer, supply-chain-industry and technology spillovers, in the US and in 10 of 11 other developed markets ([[Ali 2020 Shared Analyst Coverage Momentum Spillover]]). Three cautions apply. The best-documented link type has decayed: customer momentum roughly halved after publication (equal-weighted 1.52% to 0.62% a month; value-weighted no longer significant, 2005–2018), and part of it is a large-to-small-firm lead–lag ([[Pinchuk 2023 Customer Momentum Out of Sample]]). Graph value depends on the edge type: in a controlled volatility study, a rolling correlation graph helped slightly while sector and Granger graphs were worse than HAR ([[Wade 2026 GNN Volatility Forecasts Portfolios]]); in an LLM study, an LLM-extracted relation graph beat GICS and WikiData graphs inside the same model ([[Zhou 2026 LENS LLM Priors News Graphs]], Weak). And gains are small next to costs: cross-asset network momentum beat own momentum gross (Sharpe 1.36 vs 0.95) but was positive only up to about 3 bps per trade ([[Pu 2023 Network Momentum Across Asset Classes]]).

*(b) Do GNNs beat simple neighbour features?* The evidence does not show that they do, once the comparison is fair. Where a plain GNN is set against the same model without the graph, the graph adds little or nothing: on CSI 300, a GAT over concept graphs scored IC 0.111 against 0.113 for a GRU with no graph ([[Xu 2021 HIST Concept Graph Stock Forecasting]]); on S&P 500 volatility, an LSTM without cross-stock information had the most consistent ranking (ICIR 4.36) and graph models gained mainly when macro features were added ([[Wade 2026 GNN Volatility Forecasts Portfolios]]). The simple neighbour averages in the finance literature are strong, documented over decades, and published with t-statistics ([[Ali 2020 Shared Analyst Coverage Momentum Spillover]]; [[Cohen 2008 Economic Links Predictable Returns]]); the learned linear propagation of [[Pu 2023 Network Momentum Across Asset Classes]] is also a simple neighbour feature. The studies that report large GNN wins are the weakest on design: a gross Sharpe of 4.07 for a GAT on the analyst graph, with the simple neighbour average at 0.07 ([[Gorduza 2024 Alpha from Analyst Networks]]) contradicts the much stronger neighbour-average evidence above; others test 10 stocks ([[Li 2025 OmniGNN Multi-relational Stock GNN]]), a hand-built 48-firm graph ([[Yilki 2026 Supply Chain Propagation Text Signals]]), or compare only against persistence ([[Fanshawe 2026 Transformer GNN Equity Correlations]]). No study read compares a GNN with a gradient-boosted model given the same neighbour-average features. Outside finance, the Temporal Graph Benchmark found persistence and moving averages beat temporal GNNs on every node-level task, including token-transaction networks ([[Huang 2023 Temporal Graph Benchmark]]).

*(c) Do shocks spread along supply chains?* Yes, in real activity and in prices, with timing that depends on how visible the shock is. When a natural disaster hits a supplier, customers lose about 2 percentage points of sales growth and about 1% of equity value, almost all within 21 trading days, but only when the input is hard to replace; the loss spills over to the customer's other suppliers ([[Barrot 2016 Input Specificity Shock Propagation]]). Less visible news (a customer's ordinary return) diffuses over a month or more, mostly for bad news and small firms ([[Cohen 2008 Economic Links Predictable Returns]]); even analysts carry news across linked firms sluggishly ([[Ali 2020 Shared Analyst Coverage Momentum Spillover]]). Correlation graphs become nearly complete in crises (density 0.09 in a calm 2018 week, 0.93 in March 2020), which makes message passing close to market-wide averaging exactly when shocks spread ([[Wade 2026 GNN Volatility Forecasts Portfolios]]).

**Confidence:** High that linked-firm returns predict returns (several decades, several markets, large t-statistics) and that shocks propagate along supply chains (causal design). Medium that the effect is still large today (one post-publication test, with decay). Medium that GNNs do not beat simple neighbour features: the fair comparisons point that way, but none is Strong and none uses a GBT baseline.

### RQ1.5 – Can bursts or novelty in news, social posts or papers flag market shocks early? What lead times and false-alarm rates are reported?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-07 (12 sources: 10 new lit notes in `01 Research/Lit Notes/novelty-detection/` — 1 Strong, 5 Moderate, 4 Weak — plus [[Barrot 2016 Input Specificity Shock Propagation]] and [[Kilian 2026 LLM vs Lexicon Meme Stocks]] reused).

**Answer.** *(a) Slow signals: novelty measures the risk state months ahead.* Unusual news forecasts stress, but at monthly horizons. Unusual negative news about 50 large financial firms predicted higher company and market volatility up to six months ahead, even with lagged implied and realised volatility as controls; the aggregate volatility response peaked around four months ([[Glasserman 2019 Unusual News Forecast Market Stress]], in-sample). A rise in aggregate news entropy, from a language model retrained monthly on Reuters text so that it was available in real time, forecast lower S&P 500 returns and weaker macro data over the next year, and was the best out-of-sample predictor among a long list including VIX and EPU, though out-of-sample R² was only 0.024–0.051 (0.060–0.096 for its news-innovation part) ([[Glasserman 2023 New News is Bad News]]). A text proxy for implied volatility from WSJ front pages rose before economic disasters (+2.5 pp one-year disaster probability per s.d.) but missed the March 2000 crash ([[Manela 2017 News Implied Volatility Disasters]]). These are slow risk gauges, not alarms with a date.

*(b) Fast signals: event-level alarms have short leads and many false alarms.* Raw bursts are mostly noise: on Greek and Spanish Twitter in 2015, a general burst detector scored F1 0.23–0.33 and sentiment-only events 0.34–0.37 against intraday index jitters; a classifier trained on which bursts had moved the market raised F1 to 0.68–0.74, with warnings minutes to an hour ahead ([[Tsapeli 2017 Twitter Events Stock Market Jitters]]). For 1–3-day extreme-volatility warnings on the CSI 300, unsupervised anomaly scores on GDELT events and Chinese news lifted test AUC from 0.608 (market only) to 0.680; English news hurt, non-financial GDELT events helped, and a 100K-parameter fusion model did worse than chance ([[Qian 2026 Extreme Volatility Warning Anomaly Fusion]]). Social-media alerts for meme stocks are retrospective and unscored for false alarms ([[Gianstefani 2022 Reddit Alert System Meme Stocks]]), use synthetic social data ([[Neela 2025 AIMM Social Media Manipulation Detection]]), or reach tail-event precision of only 9–20% against base rates of 6–8% ([[Kilian 2026 LLM vs Lexicon Meme Stocks]]).

*(c) Price-only warnings fail on exogenous shocks.* Across seven BTC liquidation cascades, classic early-warning statistics did not generalise: rising variance was non-specific, an in-sample pattern inverted out of sample, and the two sudden-news (tariff) shocks showed no price precursor at all ([[Garcia Seuma 2026 Crypto Cascade Early Warning]], Strong). A correlation-graph GNN crash warner predicted "crash" on every test day (false-positive rate 1.0) and failed on COVID-19 (AUROC 0.000) ([[Neela 2025 Systemic Risk Radar Crash Warning]]). For next-day crash days, lagged VIX dominates ([[Andrew 2024 CRISIS ALERT Market Crisis ML]], with leakage). This is the strongest argument for text and event inputs: exogenous shocks are visible only once the news exists, so the lead time is bounded by the news itself. After a dated shock, its spread to linked firms plays out within about 21 trading days ([[Barrot 2016 Input Specificity Shock Propagation]]), so "detect, then propagate" is better evidenced than "predict".

*(d) Lead times and false-alarm rates reported.* Lead times: minutes to one hour (Twitter jitters), 1–3 days fixed by the label (CSI 300), about four months to the volatility peak (unusual news), 12 months (entropy, returns). False alarms: false-positive rate 0.45–0.49 for a random forest and 1.0 for GNNs (Systemic Risk Radar); 110 false alarms for 18 caught crisis days over eight years (CRISIS ALERT random forest); tail-warning precision 9–20% (meme stocks). No study read reports false alarms per year together with a lead-time distribution on real data under an out-of-sample design.

*(e) Science papers.* No study was found that tests scientific papers or preprints as early warnings of market shocks (alphaXiv and web searches, 2026-11-07). The evidence is thin; this is a finding.

**Confidence:** Medium that news novelty and unusual negative news forecast volatility and returns at monthly horizons (two studies from one group, one out-of-sample, and a separate text-uncertainty result). Low that event-level text alarms give usable lead times at acceptable false-alarm rates (small, single-market or single-crisis studies). High that price-only early-warning statistics are unreliable for exogenous shocks (one Strong study plus consistent failures). Thin for science papers.

Both can hold. [[Glasserman 2019 Unusual News Forecast Market Stress]] links them: unusual *negative* news raises later volatility. ### RQ1.6 – Which tail and uncertainty methods (quantile models, conformal prediction, extreme value theory, deep VaR) stay calibrated in crises?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-07 (10 new lit notes in `01 Research/Lit Notes/tail-risk/`: 3 Strong, 7 Moderate, 0 Weak).

**Answer.** *(a) Nothing stays calibrated at the onset of a shock; the question is how fast a method recovers.* In the 55-day COVID window (Feb–Apr 2020), 95% intervals for the S&P 500 covered only 80% (conformal TCP and GARCH), 65% (rolling quantile regression and historical simulation) and 56% (adaptive conformal inference, ACI) ([[Aich 2025 Temporal Conformal Prediction Risk]]). On the CRSP index in 2018–2024, every uncalibrated base model exceeded a 99% VaR too often: LightGBM quantile regression 4.05%, GARCH-t 1.94%, historical simulation 1.83% ([[Schmitt 2026 Conformal Calibration Portfolio VaR]], Strong). Exogenous shocks are visible to price-based tail models only after the first large move; this matches the RQ1.5 finding that price-only warnings fail on news shocks.

*(b) Volatility filtering plus a fat-tailed innovation model is the core that works.* GARCH filtering with a generalised Pareto tail on the residuals (conditional EVT) passed violation tests at 95%, 99% and 99.5% on five series including the 1987 crash; GARCH-normal was rejected at 99.5% on all five, and unconditional EVT was violated in runs during stress ([[McNeil 2000 Conditional EVT Tail Risk]]). Normal innovations are "useless" for expected shortfall in that study, and normal-output neural networks also fail 1% tests ([[Michankow 2025 Deep Networks Return Distributions]]). With a GARCH-t base, stress-quintile exceedances stayed at or below target under every calibrator, and DQ tests passed on 13–14 of 16 portfolios ([[Schmitt 2026 Conformal Calibration Portfolio VaR]]).

*(c) Conformal calibration helps, but as a layer on a conditional model, not a substitute.* Conformal wrappers restore average coverage, but feedback methods (ACI, DtACI, conformal PID) widen only after violations, so their misses concentrate in stress: with a historical-simulation base, stress-quintile exceedance was 1.71–2.29% for feedback methods against 0.86% for recency- and regime-weighted calibration (1% target), at a higher capital cost. No calibrator rescued the historical-simulation base from dynamic-quantile test rejection ([[Schmitt 2026 Conformal Calibration Portfolio VaR]]). Adaptive look-back windows cut joint VaR–ES loss in the GFC (3.25% vs 3.66% for a fixed 250-day window) ([[Li 2026 Adaptive Window Selection Risk]]).

*(d) Deep and foundation models reach parity with strong econometric baselines, rarely more, except with large cross-sections.* Fine-tuned TimesFM had better violation ratios than GAS and GARCH on 91 S&P 100 stocks (2015–2023) but only matched GAS on quantile loss; zero-shot TimesFM was clearly worse, and at 5–10% levels it did not beat historical simulation ([[Goel 2024 TimesFM Value-at-Risk Forecasting]]). LSTM mixture density networks showed no superiority over GARCH(1,1) and were seed-sensitive ([[Herrig 2025 LSTM Mixture Density VaR]]). Skewed-t LSTM and CNN outputs were competitive with the best GARCH per index ([[Michankow 2025 Deep Networks Return Distributions]]). The exception is pooled models trained on many assets and characteristics: a quantile network beat GARCH, random forests and boosted trees on tail-quantile loss for monthly stock returns ([[Barunik 2024 Quantile Neural Networks Return Distributions]]), and a pooled VaR–ES model beat per-asset GARCH and GAS with gains attributed to more data, not more parameters ([[Zhang 2026 ReSGA Large Tail Risk Model]], Strong). Both are monthly; neither reports crisis-subperiod calibration in the pages read.

*(e) Combining helps.* Across 90 econometric VaR/ES methods on six indices, trimmed means, probability averaging and performance-weighted combinations did best; the pool as a whole underestimated risk, and small diverse pools with shorter estimation windows improved results ([[Taylor 2025 Combining VaR and ES Forecasts]], Strong).

**Confidence:** High that volatility-filtered, fat-tailed models (conditional EVT, GARCH-t/skew-t, or equivalents) are the baseline to beat and that normal-tail models fail at 99% and for ES. High that no method read stays calibrated in the first weeks of an exogenous shock. Medium that conformal calibration with recency weighting improves stress-period coverage when the base model adapts slowly (one Strong study, one Moderate). Medium that deep and foundation models reach parity with GARCH/GAS at daily horizons; Low that they beat them in crises. Medium that pooled cross-sectional models beat per-asset models at monthly horizons.

### RQ1.7 – Does multimodal fusion beat the best single modality? Compare late, early, cross-attention and mixture-of-experts fusion, and note when each helps

**Status:** answered 2026-11-07 (14 new lit notes in `01 Research/Lit Notes/fusion/`: 1 Strong, 5 Moderate, 8 Weak; plus [[Tan 2024 Are LMs Useful for Time Series]] reused).

**Answer.** *(a) Fusion does not reliably beat the best single modality; the stronger the single-modality model, the smaller the gain.* Across 16 text-paired forecasting tasks, unimodal models were best on 6–7 of 16 and the difference from aligned multimodal models was not significant (paired t-test p = 0.79 for MSE); the multimodal gain correlated 0.90 with how weak the unimodal model was ([[Zhang 2025 When Multimodality Helps TS Forecasting]]). Naive addition or concatenation of text embeddings often fell below the text-free model and sometimes diverged ([[Lee 2026 Constrained Fusion Text Time Series]]). On financial news for 50 S&P 500 tickers, a mutual-information test found no information in the text about daily returns beyond history for any ticker, and a repeat-last-value forecast beat a 14.3B-parameter mixture-of-experts model ([[Zhou 2026 When Context Routing Helps Fusion]]). In small stock-movement benchmarks, adding tweet or news embeddings to an attentive LSTM lowered accuracy on three of four datasets ([[Zong 2024 MSGCA Gated Cross-Attention Fusion]]), and on Russian stocks news embeddings cut price MAPE but lowered direction accuracy (52.0% → 46.97–49.80%) ([[Khubiyev 2025 Multimodal Russian Stock Prediction]]). Pretrained LLMs reading numbers add little ([[Tan 2024 Are LMs Useful for Time Series]]); GPT-4o did better on text alone than with prices added ([[Koval 2025 Modality-Specific Experts Text and TS]]).

*(b) Where fusion helps in finance, gains are small and come from text that is complementary to the numeric inputs.* With ~200 quantitative factors and post-cutoff news (DeBERTa, test 2023–2024), concatenation fusion raised long-short Sharpe from 1.26 to 1.64 (North America) and 1.46 to 1.70 (Europe) but lowered it from 2.96 to 2.21 in Emerging Markets, where news repeated what factors already held; news alone was useless (Sharpe 0.18, −1.57, 0.21) ([[Guo 2025 Factors and Newsflow Fusion Returns]]). A Granger-gated macro-event model improved on its own price-only branch by only 1.5–3.8% MSE ([[Zhang 2026 GS-Fuse Granger-Gated Event Fusion]]). FinBERT sentiment added a little at earnings announcements (macro-F1 0.359 → 0.390) on a very small test ([[Noseda 2026 Earnings Day Multimodal Direction]]). The largest claimed fusion gain (net Sharpe 1.51 vs 0.46 price-only and 0.82 text-only, 2020–2024) uses Llama3-8B on news that predates its training cutoff and no GBT or factor baseline ([[Koval 2025 Modality-Specific Experts Text and TS]]), so its size is not trusted.

*(c) Fusion type: simple or constrained fusion beats unconstrained attention.* Concatenation beat summation and attention in all three universes; attention fusion was worst each time ([[Guo 2025 Factors and Newsflow Fusion Returns]]). Gating, FiLM, orthogonal injection and a low-rank residual adapter beat naive fusion and beat the text-free model on most backbones ([[Lee 2026 Constrained Fusion Text Time Series]]). Plain cross-attention was hurt by noisy text, while gated cross-attention led by the price modality recovered small gains ([[Zong 2024 MSGCA Gated Cross-Attention Fusion]]); cross-attention and text-as-variable were slightly harmful when the context was uninformative ([[Zhou 2026 When Context Routing Helps Fusion]]); cross-modal attention in early LLM layers and shared parameters across modalities did worse than separate modality parameters with late cross-attention ([[Koval 2025 Modality-Specific Experts Text and TS]]). The common pattern: keep prices or factors as the primary branch and let text in through a gate or residual that can shut it off. Early versus late placement had no consistent winner across datasets ([[Zhang 2025 When Multimodality Helps TS Forecasting]]).

*(d) Mixture-of-experts helps as a robustness device, not as extra capacity.* A mixture over a factor-only expert and a fusion expert, trained with decoupled updates, was the most robust across universes, roughly matching the best single design in each ([[Guo 2025 Factors and Newsflow Fusion Returns]]). For cross-sectional volatility (1,027 US stocks, 30 walk-forward quarters), regime variables helped only when they routed residual experts on a frozen base model; feeding the same variables as inputs lowered accuracy and collapsed training (30 of 30 seeds), a standard MoE collapsed in 24 of 30 seeds, and soft routing beat every hard regime split — but the total gain was small (IC 0.547 vs 0.542 for a capacity-matched MLP and 0.532 for HAR) ([[Ye 2026 Regime-Gated Residual MoE Volatility]], Strong).

*(e) Many reported fusion gains are evaluation artefacts.* On Time-MMD, swapping the text for empty, shuffled, cross-domain or oracle text moved error by under 0.5% for three architectures; the published lift came from a numeric column routed through the same fusion path ([[Sridhar 2026 Text Sensitivity Audit Multimodal TS]]). The XForecast competition weights test cases by whether the news agreed with the realised move, a hindsight-weighted metric ([[Lee 2026 XForecast News-Grounded Challenge]]). A commodity-shock model reporting AUC 0.94 used yearly news summaries written by an LLM after the fact and full-sample normalisation ([[Ghali 2025 Commodity Price Shocks Fusion]]). A FTSE study's F1 edge came from recall in a rising market, with precision (51.0%) below random forest ([[Pei 2025 Cross-Modal Temporal Fusion FTSE]]).

**Confidence:** High that fusion does not reliably beat a strong single modality and that naive concatenation or unconstrained cross-attention can hurt (several independent benchmarks agree, incl. a financial-news null). High that text-on/text-off ablations alone do not show that text content is used. Medium that gated, residual or mixture designs that keep the price/factor branch intact give small, consistent gains when the text is complementary. Medium that mixture-of-experts mainly buys stability (one Strong study, volatility only). Low on the economic size of text–price fusion gains in equities.

The stronger and cleaner the numeric baseline, the smaller the text gain, which matches [[Zhang 2025 When Multimodality Helps TS Forecasting]]. **What would change this answer:** a post-cutoff or point-in-time LLM study (RQ1.2) that fuses news with a GBT on factors and price features, with shuffled-text placebos, costs and significance tests; a direct comparison of concatenation, gated and cross-attention fusion on the same clean financial dataset; evidence on whether gated fusion helps most in stress periods.

### RQ1.8 – What does the literature say about predicting versus detecting rare, unprecedented events? Where is the theoretical limit?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-07 (8 new lit notes: 0 Strong, 5 Moderate, 3 Weak; plus [[Garcia Seuma 2026 Crypto Cascade Early Warning]], [[Glasserman 2019 Unusual News Forecast Market Stress]] and [[Andrew 2024 CRISIS ALERT Market Crisis ML]] reused).

**Answer.** *(a) Endogenous, boom-built crises are predictable in probability over years, not in timing.* Across 42 countries (1950–2016), joint credit and asset-price booms (the "R-zone") raise the three-year crisis probability to 36.8–45.3% from a ~12% base rate, with the signal on average 2.9 years before onset; but within-country R² is 6–8% in sample and 0.9–4.5% out of sample, the business R-zone misses 60% of crises and more than half its alarms are false, and "double-dip" crises are missed altogether ([[Greenwood 2022 Predictable Financial Crises]]). In recursive quasi-real-time tests for 15 advanced economies, a simple logit on credit, asset-price and external-imbalance indicators beat random forests, SVMs, kNN and trees (relative usefulness 0.605 vs −0.186 to 0.126); the ML methods fit near-perfectly in sample and often did worse than a naive forecast out of sample ([[Beutel 2019 ML vs Logit Banking Crisis Warning]]). Even the true model would miss about a third of crises in a DSGE simulation, because crises can only be predicted in probability (same source, p. 21). Slow-moving text novelty works the same way: unusual negative news forecasts higher volatility months ahead, peaking around four months, in-sample ([[Glasserman 2019 Unusual News Forecast Market Stress]]).

*(b) Generic "tipping-point" warnings in prices do not work for markets.* Before the 1929, 1987, 2000 and 2008 crashes there was no critical slowing down (no rising autocorrelation) in five major indices; detrended variance did rise before every major Dow crash, but the same signal fired seven times with no crash in 115 years and its lead time ran from 3 months to 5 years ([[Guttal 2016 No Critical Slowing Down Before Crashes]]). Across seven crypto liquidation cascades, an in-sample warning pattern inverted out of sample and the two news-driven (tariff) shocks had no price precursor at all ([[Garcia Seuma 2026 Crypto Cascade Early Warning]]). The best-known crash-timing model (log-periodic power law) fits its own parameter ranges for only 7 of 11 Hang Seng crashes, fails on two of the three crashes not used to set those ranges, and is very sensitive to one parameter ([[Bree 2010 LPPL Crash Test Hang Seng]]). Price/VIX-based next-day crisis classifiers trade hit rate against false alarms (18/19 crisis days with 110 false alarms, or 12/19 with 4) and leaned on lagged VIX ([[Andrew 2024 CRISIS ALERT Market Crisis ML]]).

*(c) Truly unprecedented events are outside what learned models can extrapolate to.* A deep weather model trained without the strongest storms matched the full model on ordinary skill metrics but could not forecast Category 5 storms, predicting moderate ones instead (silent false negatives); it did generalise when similar storms had been seen in another basin ([[Sun 2024 AI Weather Gray Swan Cyclones]]). Weighted losses cannot fix events absent from training (same source).

*(d) Where the theoretical limit sits.* For any model, the minimum error for an extreme-event forecast is set by the information in the observables; error splits into initial-condition noise, unobserved variables and model error, and as horizons lengthen unobserved variables dominate; rarer events are harder ([[Yuan 2024 Limits Extreme Event Forecasting Chaos]]). With finite samples there is also an estimation-error floor: a weak or negative out-of-sample R² does not prove a target is unpredictable — for monthly US market returns the implied population R² is at least ~20% while realised R²_OOS is negative ([[Chen 2025 Limits to Machine Learning]]). Together: once a model is near the information bound, gains must come from new observables (text, order flow, linked-firm data), not a bigger model; and with tens of crisis events, a mapping that may exist cannot be learned reliably.

*(e) Evaluation itself is fragile at low prevalence.* F1 and MCC thresholds drift towards "never alarm" and AUC saturates as events get rarer, so rankings reflect prevalence rather than tail skill ([[Nikolopoulos 2025 Rare-Event-Stable Evaluation Metrics]]); standard skill metrics also hid the gray-swan failure in [[Sun 2024 AI Weather Gray Swan Cyclones]].

**Confidence:** High that the timing of crashes and exogenous shocks cannot be predicted from prices, and that flexible ML does not beat simple models for crisis prediction with so few events (independent results across banking crises, equity indices, crypto and LPPL). High that build-up of endogenous fragility (credit and price booms, news novelty) raises crisis probability over months to years. Medium that deep models cannot extrapolate to larger-than-seen shocks in markets (strong evidence comes from weather, one model). Low on the size of the theoretical limit for markets: the bounds are conceptual and cannot yet be computed for crashes.

[[Chen 2025 Limits to Machine Learning]] (more predictability than OOS tests show) and [[Beutel 2019 ML vs Logit Banking Crisis Warning]] (flexible models fail OOS) are consistent: both say finite samples, not absence of signal, limit flexible models.

### RQ1.9 – Is there evidence for reinforcement learning, MAML-inspired meta-learning (first-order MAML, Reptile, meta-learned adapters, in-context adaptation) or search-based planning (such as A\*) in forecasting or trading decisions?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-07 (13 new lit notes in `01 Research/Lit Notes/rl-planning/`: 2 Strong, 4 Moderate, 7 Weak; plus [[Nguyen 2026 Volatility-Clustering Adaptation]] reused).

**Answer.** *(a) Reinforcement learning for trading decisions has no robust out-of-sample evidence of beating simple rules after costs.* The best-designed tests point the same way. An end-to-end transformer policy trained on a differentiable Sharpe loss (the policy-function form of policy-gradient RL) only matched equal weighting on 16 liquid futures over 2011–2024 (net Sharpe 0.54 vs 0.52; bootstrap probability of beating 1/N 0.59), time-series momentum won in rates and FX, its one Bonferroni-significant result (equities) was mostly market beta, and mixture-of-experts, extra features and ensembles did not help; only seed averaging did ([[Pollok 2026 End-to-End Policies vs Simple Rules]]). In a staged crypto benchmark, both RL methods lost money in the held-out 2025 backtest, the backtest ranking of all 32 methods nearly reversed in prospective paper trading, and none of the four survivors beat Bitcoin buy-and-hold with real money ([[Yu 2026 AI Crypto Backtest to Live Gap]]). Five standard pre-trained agents (PPO, SAC, A2C, TD3, DDPG) on 28 Dow stocks had lower Sharpe than buy-and-hold in 2023–2025 (0.85–1.13 vs 1.18) ([[Go 2026 FinPILOT Inference-Time Planning RL]]). A survey of 167 RL-in-finance papers finds that agents "generally" beat their authors' benchmarks, but flags unequal information sets, Sharpe ratios of 21 and 218 that point to same-bar look-ahead, survivorship-biased tests and little factor adjustment ([[Pippas 2025 RL in Quantitative Finance Survey]]). Papers that report large DRL wins share those flaws: high market beta (0.83–1.24), survivorship-biased mega-caps, no costs and no seeds ([[Wang 2026 DRL Equity Actor-Critic Forward Retraining]]); hand-picked hyperparameters and four variants per asset, with the same algorithm swinging from best to worst by network type ([[Maskiewicz 2025 Can AI Trade the Stock Market]]). Hyperparameter sensitivity is itself a main finding: retraining cut DDPG's annual return from 55.5% to 29.8% ([[Wang 2026 DRL Equity Actor-Critic Forward Retraining]]), and screening agents by the probability of backtest overfitting rejected walk-forward-tuned PPO and SAC ([[Gort 2022 DRL Crypto Backtest Overfitting]]); that seed paper's "winning" agent still lost 35% in its two-month test, and its risk switch was set from the test period.

*(b) MAML-style meta-learning and adaptation: weak evidence of gains, consistent evidence that light adaptation beats heavy adaptation.* MAML with regime-clustered tasks raised next-day ICs on unseen stocks in the 2020 rebound (e.g. 0.026 vs 0.016) but plain pre-training won some cells ([[Liu 2025 Robust Meta-Learning Zero-Shot Financial]]); Reptile-style first-order meta-learning transferred some structure to newly listed stocks (China MCC 8–11 vs 7–8 for transfer learning) at 51–57% accuracy, and plain Reptile's trading strategy lost 79% a year in a 2.5-month test ([[Wang 2023 Meta-Stock Sub-new Stock Meta-Learning]]). Both are single short windows without costs. For adaptation without meta-learning: refreshing batch-normalisation statistics on recent unlabeled data gave small DM-significant gains while gradient updates of normalisation parameters made SPY and QQQ forecasts significantly worse ([[Wu 2026 Test-Time Adaptation Non-stationary Markets]]); naive fine-tuning of Kronos can underperform the frozen model ([[Nguyen 2026 Volatility-Clustering Adaptation]]); and fine-tuning, replay, EWC and constrained activations all performed the same as plain rolling retraining for RL portfolio agents, with forgetting near zero ([[Pan 2026 ReCAP Regime-Adaptive Continual Learning]]). That paper's own regime library of policy vectors added about 15 points of average performance and held at +10 bps, but on survivorship-biased universes with no significance tests. In-context adaptation: choosing an LLM's demonstrations by estimated volatility regime cut high-volatility error 27% against GJR-GARCH, but HAR stayed best in calm periods and memorisation was uncontrolled ([[Asaad 2026 Regime-Aware In-Context Volatility]]). No study of meta-learned adapters on financial data was found.

*(c) Search-based planning: no study of A\* for forecasting or trading was found; the nearest evidence supports planning only where a model of the dynamics exists and the objective is non-convex.* AlphaZero-style tree search found the optimal hedge in 85–100% of runs where gradient deep hedging found it in 0–26%, in simulated markets with non-convex costs or constraints; with convex costs deep hedging is fine ([[Maggiolo 2025 Deep Hedging Non-Convexity AlphaZero]]). Model-predictive planning at decision time — adapting a frozen RL actor against forecast price paths from a weak XGBoost forecaster (R² 0.016) — lifted DJ30 Sharpe from 0.85–1.13 to 1.22–1.55 and improved 27 of 30 settings, while convex optimisers fed the same forecasts did worse than passive ([[Go 2026 FinPILOT Inference-Time Planning RL]]). Planning adds value as a decision layer on top of a forecast; it is not a source of forecasting edge.

**Confidence:** High that deep RL has no robust evidence of beating 1/N, buy-and-hold or time-series momentum after costs, and that reported RL wins are fragile to seeds, hyperparameters and survivorship (two Strong studies, one Moderate study and a 167-paper survey agree; supportive papers share identifiable flaws). Medium that light, gradient-free or plain-retraining adaptation is as good as or better than heavier continual- or meta-learning (three independent results, none Strong). Low on any benefit of MAML/Reptile or in-context adaptation for markets (Weak, single-window studies), and Low on search-based planning (one simulation study, one backtest; nothing on A\*).

### RQ1.10 – How should numerical market data be represented for sequence models: fixed bins, learned codebooks (vector quantisation), hybrid tokenizers, or continuous features fed directly? What does discretisation lose, and do token models beat continuous-input models?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-07 (11 new lit notes in `01 Research/Lit Notes/price-time-series/`: 0 Strong, 6 Moderate, 5 Weak; plus [[Shi 2025 Kronos Financial Foundation Model]] reused and updated with its tokenizer ablation). Stop rule not fully met (stopped at 12; the last three sources still added findings; top-up list in Contradictions and evidence gaps).

**Answer.** *(a) No representation wins everywhere; the evidence splits by where the discretisation happens.* Two finance studies point in opposite directions. In Kronos, at comparable size, a discrete hierarchical-token model beat continuous autoregressive variants trained with MSE or a Student-t mixture likelihood on price IC (0.0431 vs 0.0212 and 0.0179), return IC (0.0665 vs 0.0416 and 0.0356) and volatility MAE (0.0384 vs 0.0565 and 0.0464) in one post-2024 window without tests; larger vocabularies (2^14 → 2^20) improved both reconstruction and forecasts ([[Shi 2025 Kronos Financial Foundation Model]]). In VAIOM, a decoder on 1-hour FX bars with the *output* kept as 16 volatility-normalised return buckets did better with *continuous* inputs than with tokenized inputs (validation NLL 3.1536 vs 3.1633 bits/event), and the hybrid continuous-input model beat Frequency, Markov and single-bar LightGBM baselines on 2025 test data by 0.018–0.043 bits/event across three seeds, with block-bootstrap intervals excluding zero ([[Ma 2026 VAIOM Continuous-Input Discrete-Output]]). Read together: discretising the **output** into a categorical distribution has support in both; discretising the **input** does not, in the one finance study that isolated it, though its input fields were not matched. Outside finance, the earliest controlled study found binning "almost always" helps but mainly through the output, with input bins mattering less and the effect reversing for DeepAR ([[Rabanser 2020 Effectiveness of Discretization Forecasting]]); a VQ-VAE codebook beat continuous patches with the forecaster held fixed ([[Talukder 2024 TOTEM Tokenized Time Series]]); and in mixed event-and-number sequences, binning a value and then projecting it continuously ("combined binning") was the robust default, while continuous values conditioned on event type were most precise ([[Montgomery 2026 Numeric Value Encoding Transformers]]).

*(b) What discretisation loses, and what it buys.* Fixed bins cap the representable range and merge nearby values: Chronos' 4,094 uniform bins on ±15× the context mean cannot represent spikes beyond that range, cannot extrapolate strong trends, and lose precision when the scale is large relative to variance ([[Ansari 2024 Chronos Language of Time Series]]); StockGPT clips daily returns at ±100% and uses 50-bp bins ([[Mai 2024 StockGPT Generative Stock Returns]]). Bin design matters most when the vocabulary is small: at 512 tokens, standard scaling with uniform bins gave MASE 2.342 against 4.868 for the Chronos default, and the schemes converge by 4,096 ([[Roger 2025 Small Vocabularies Tokenization TS]]). Controlled mechanism tests show the trade-off is structural: quantised embeddings damp limited outliers and learn fine-scale patterns better, continuous embeddings let large values dominate, and cross-entropy commits to modes rather than regressing to the mean, at the cost of occasionally forecasting an outlier ([[Yu 2025 Implicit Biases TSFM Design Choices]]). As a regulariser of learned state, a 512-code VQ bottleneck was the most important component in a cross-sectional stock-ranking model (removing it dropped S&P 500 RankIC from 0.0141 to −0.0024), though absolute ICs are tiny ([[Kim 2026 PRISM-VQ Discrete Latent Factors]]). Learned motif tokens (BPE over coarse bins) compress smooth series 2–23× and beat per-value tokens, but have not been tried on near-white-noise returns ([[Goetz 2026 Byte Pair Encoding Time Series]]).

*(c) Do token models beat continuous-input models?* Not demonstrably. On 27 unseen general datasets, token-based Chronos (0.823 relative MASE) sat level with continuous-input PatchTST (0.810) and AutoETS (0.838), and on the one FX dataset it was worse than a random walk (MASE 2.375 vs 1.874) ([[Ansari 2024 Chronos Language of Time Series]]). The Chronos developers have since moved to continuous patches with a quantile head (Chronos-Bolt, Chronos-2), and Chronos-2 leads fev-bench, but the original token model is not in that comparison ([[Ansari 2026 Chronos-2 Multivariate Foundation Model]]). Token models do show real signal in finance (StockGPT's monthly long-short ~13% a year with ~15% factor alpha, 2001–2023), but no finance study compares a token model against an equally tuned continuous-input model plus a GARCH/HAR or GBT baseline.

**Confidence:** Medium that a categorical output over volatility-normalised return buckets is a sound target (supported in Kronos, VAIOM and StockGPT; consistent with the mechanism study). Low on input representation (two finance studies disagree; non-finance controlled studies mostly favour tokens but on smooth, seasonal data). Medium that bin design (scaling, range, vocabulary size) matters most at small vocabularies and that fixed uniform bins mishandle tails and range. Low that token models beat continuous-input models for returns or volatility (no matched finance test).

These results are **not directly comparable**: different data, metrics and, above all, different contrasts. Kronos changes the input and the output together and compares against continuous *regression/density* heads; VAIOM holds the categorical output fixed and changes only the input. Both are consistent with "a categorical output helps"; they disagree only on the input, and VAIOM's input fields were not matched (its own caveat). Cite Kronos and StockGPT for "token models carry signal in finance", VAIOM for "continuous input with a bucketed output", Chronos and Roger for tokenizer failure modes, Yu for mechanisms, Montgomery for hybrid binning. ### RQ1.11 – How can text and event streams tell independent corroboration from repetition of one report? How are bots, coordinated campaigns, misinformation and source reliability detected, and does filtering them improve market signals?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-08 (11 new lit notes: 0 Strong, 6 Moderate, 5 Weak, in `01 Research/Lit Notes/novelty-detection/`, `sentiment/` and `llm-text/`; plus [[Neela 2025 AIMM Social Media Manipulation Detection]] and [[Paredes Amorin 2026 Conditional Sentiment Aluminum]] reused). Stop rule not fully met: stopped at 13; of the last three new sources, [[Wang 2015 Crowds on Wall Street]] added one nuance (reliability ranks persist only on a curated platform) and [[Huang 2019 Trust Filters Twitter Stock Prediction]] added nothing new. Top-up list in Contradictions and evidence gaps.

**Answer.** *Terms.* "Repetition" means a later item that re-uses content from earlier items (a reprint, a syndicated copy, a retweet, or a recombination of several earlier stories). "Corroboration" means an independent source confirming the same fact. "Source reliability" means how often a source's past items turned out right or genuine.

*(a) Telling corroboration from repetition.* **Finance studies measure repetition well and corroboration hardly at all.** The standard tool is textual overlap with earlier items about the same firm, using only past items (point-in-time). Tetlock's staleness (Jaccard similarity to the previous ten stories) shows the market reacts much less to stale news (413 bp smaller initial move, bottom vs top decile, equal-weighted), but the move it does make partly reverses next week (−26 bp, t ≈ −4.8), driven by individual investors ([[Tetlock 2011 Stale News Reprint]], Moderate 6). Fedyk and Hodson split old news into reprints (one source) and recombinations (several earlier sources stitched together): the market discounts reprints, but over-reacts to recombinations (≈13 bp larger next-day absolute returns per 10% shift from reprint to recombination), which then reverse; professionals also rated recombinations as more novel, and the effect grew over 2001–2014 ([[Fedyk 2023 When Market Identifies Old News]], Moderate 6). So near-duplicate removal is not enough: a recombination of old facts from many outlets looks like corroboration but carries no new information. No finance study found here tests a measure of *independent* confirmation (distinct original reporters, provenance chains) against a forecast. Outside finance, the clearest evidence is a failure case: in news-reading forecasters, five fabricated, mutually consistent articles per question looked like corroboration, shifted probabilities by +0.13 to +0.23 net of a placebo and flipped 69–77% of forecasts; publisher labels made no difference (Reuters vs obscure domain Δp +0.215 vs +0.212), and stricter relevance filtering made things worse because it raised the fake share of what survived ([[Lu 2026 Corroboration Illusion News Poisoning]], Moderate 9).

*(b) Bots, coordinated campaigns and misinformation: how they are detected.* Three families of method appear. (1) *Account behaviour:* rule-based activity limits (night-time posting, >10 posts a day, repeated identical posts) ([[Fan 2020 Social Media Bots Stock Markets]]), group-similarity detectors such as "digital DNA" (71% of accounts behind the 100 largest cashtag bursts flagged; 94% of later Twitter suspensions also flagged) ([[Cresci 2018 Cashtag Piggybacking Bots]], Weak), and de-spamming by keeping one post per user-stock-day ([[Zhou 2025 Expert Opinion Social Media Stock Prediction]]). (2) *Content and co-mention patterns:* bursts made mostly of retweets (60% vs 23% baseline) pairing a few large-cap cashtags with many unrelated OTC tickers ([[Cresci 2018 Cashtag Piggybacking Bots]]); message classifiers on pump channels (F1 0.98, but on a random split) ([[Bolz 2025 Real-Time Pump-and-Dump Detection]], Weak). (3) *Linguistic deception scores:* a LIWC authenticity score missed ~90% of SEC-identified fake articles and left ~64% of articles unclassifiable ([[Kogan 2023 Social Media Financial News Manipulation]], Moderate 7); perplexity filters catch default LLM-written fakes but are beaten by sampling at higher temperature ([[Lu 2026 Corroboration Illusion News Poisoning]]). Coordinated-campaign *prediction* is short-range: order-book anomalies ranked the pumped coin in the top 5 of 51 in 56% of 43 events 20 seconds ahead, 19% one minute ahead ([[Bolz 2025 Real-Time Pump-and-Dump Detection]]); a fused Reddit-plus-price manipulation score was beaten by a price-only anomaly rule (AUC 0.863 vs 0.647) and used synthetic social data ([[Neela 2025 AIMM Social Media Manipulation Detection]], Weak).

*(c) Do contaminated or manipulated streams distort market signals?* Yes, in measurable ways. Fake Seeking Alpha articles raised abnormal volume >50% and volatility ≈40% and lifted small-firm prices (up to ≈13% at ~60 days) before full reversal; after the SEC case became public, investors reacted ≈4–5% less to *all* articles (−35% for small firms, −30% for high-retail stocks), while placebo outlets showed no change ([[Kogan 2023 Social Media Financial News Manipulation]]). Bot tweets (~5% of FTSE 100 tweets) related to volatility and volume more weakly than human tweets and carried no reliable return information; positive bot spikes were followed by negative next-week returns ([[Fan 2020 Social Media Bots Stock Markets]], Weak). Repetition without new content produced over-reaction and reversal (Tetlock; Fedyk and Hodson above).

*(d) Does filtering improve market signals?* **Some evidence that it helps, mostly in-sample or small; no clean test against a strong price model shows a large gain.** Filtering by source track record: crowd StockTwits sentiment predicted next-day direction worse than chance (47.6%), users selected by a two-year, regime-spanning record (after de-spamming) were right 72.8% of the time, but these signals covered ~4% of stock-days and added little once combined with a strong price model (S&P 500 IC 7.15% → 8.26%; Nasdaq 100 7.83% → 7.98%) ([[Zhou 2025 Expert Opinion Social Media Stock Prediction]], Moderate 6). Earlier track-record rules failed out of sample (users >80% accurate on 20 posts were 54% accurate later) (same source). Reputation ranks persisted out of sample on edited SeekingAlpha (correlation ≈0.12) but not on open StockTwits ([[Wang 2015 Crowds on Wall Street]], Moderate 6); trust-weighted Twitter sentiment beat unweighted sentiment in one six-month, cost-free test (ROI 4.26% vs 2.81%) ([[Huang 2019 Trust Filters Twitter Stock Prediction]], Weak). Filtering by source quality: Reuters headlines (Sharpe 0.80) beat Dow Jones (0.18) and China News Service (0.15), and reports of events that had happened beat forward-looking ones (0.62 vs −0.01), in one data-mined aluminium study ([[Paredes Amorin 2026 Conditional Sentiment Aluminum]], Weak). Filtering by age: zeroing filing-derived news signals older than eight weeks changed decisions but not net returns beyond noise (paired 95% intervals all include zero), and market-data-only RL beat every news variant ([[Song 2026 When News Gets Old]], Weak). Bot removal itself has not been tested as a forecasting ablation in the sources found.

**Confidence:** Medium that repetition is measurable point-in-time by textual overlap with earlier items and that the market over-reacts to repeated and especially recombined old news, followed by reversal (two large Moderate studies agree; both in-sample). Medium that bots, coordinated campaigns and fake articles are common enough to distort volume, volatility and small-cap prices (several studies, different platforms). Low that filtering by bot status, source track record or source quality improves forecasts over a strong price baseline (small or in-sample gains only). Thin evidence on detecting *independent* corroboration (provenance) in finance; the one rigorous study is outside finance and shows that labels and volume can be faked cheaply.

### RQ1.12 – How fast does information move from first observation to wide dissemination to price? How do studies measure what is already priced in (options, overseas markets, pre-announcement drift)?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-08 (8 new lit notes: 0 Strong, 6 Moderate, 2 Weak, in `01 Research/Lit Notes/price-time-series/`, `novelty-detection/` and `tail-risk/`, tag `information-diffusion`; plus [[Cohen 2008 Economic Links Predictable Returns]], [[Tetlock 2011 Stale News Reprint]], [[Jiao 2020 Social Media News Media Stock Market]] and [[Song 2026 When News Gets Old]] reused). Stop rule not fully met: stopped at 12; the last three new sources each added a finding. Top-up list in Contradictions and evidence gaps. **Top-up 2026-11-08 (session 15):** 6 lit notes from the top-up list ([[Chordia 2018 Low-Latency Trading Macro Announcements]], [[Pan 2006 Option Volume Future Stock Prices]], [[Rapach 2013 International Return Predictability US Role]], [[Siliverstovs 2015 US Role International Predictability Recessions]]: Moderate; [[Hong 2000 Bad News Travels Slowly]], [[Busse 2002 Market Efficiency in Real Time]]: Weak, both read from summaries/abstracts only). RQ1.12 now has 18 sources, above the 15-source cap (see Assumptions); the top-up removed the 'thin' rating on overseas lead-lag and added two findings (sub-second absorption of macro news; slower diffusion of bad news in small, low-coverage stocks).

**Answer.** *Terms.* "First observation" is the earliest public trace of an event (a post, a wire, a filing); "wide dissemination" is repeated coverage across outlets; "priced in" means the price has already moved in the news direction before the item a model would read.

*(a) How fast does information move to price?* **It depends on the kind of news and on liquidity: seconds for scheduled numeric news in liquid stocks, days to months for news that must be connected to other firms.** For macro-announcement surprises, SPY and E-mini prices respond within a tenth of a second (journal version: five milliseconds), most of the 10-second move is done in 2 seconds, and fast-trading profits (≈$19k–50k per event before data fees) fell to about zero by 2014 ([[Chordia 2018 Low-Latency Trading Macro Announcements]], Moderate 6). Widely broadcast analyst views on CNBC were priced within seconds; positive reports within a minute, negative ones over about 15 minutes ([[Busse 2002 Market Efficiency in Real Time]], Weak 5, abstract only). After-hours earnings announcements produced a price jump in 91% of cases for the 50 most-traded stocks (96% after 2016), and a post-announcement trading rule earned nothing after 2016 once spreads or a 5-second delay were charged ([[Christensen 2026 Warp Speed Jumps After Earnings]], Moderate 9). For scheduled macro releases, futures started moving about 30 minutes *before* release in 9 of 20 market-moving announcements, with 36–49% of the total adjustment happening early; drift was stronger where release procedures were less secure ([[Kurov 2019 Price Drift Before Macro News]], Moderate 6). Across 1.68M tagged firm news events (2023–2026), most of the news-direction move had happened by the close of publication day (pooled running total +0.58% at day 0 vs +0.20% at day +20) ([[Kargarzadeh 2026 When Is News Priced In]], Weak 5). Slower channels remain: momentum is strongest in small, low-analyst-coverage stocks and especially among past losers, consistent with bad news diffusing slowly (1.13% vs 0.72% a month, low vs high residual coverage, 1980–96) ([[Hong 2000 Bad News Travels Slowly]], Weak 5, summary only); supplier stocks under-react to news about their customers for about a month ([[Cohen 2008 Economic Links Predictable Returns]]); repeated (stale) news moves prices through individual investors and partly reverses over the next week ([[Tetlock 2011 Stale News Reprint]]); heavy social-media coverage is followed by higher volatility and turnover, consistent with echoing old news ([[Jiao 2020 Social Media News Media Stock Market]]). Which *channel* is first is unstable: news outlets led X by a median 21.6 minutes on encyclopedic events but the two tied within seconds on prediction-market events, and a third of first reports came from other platforms ([[Bazyari 2026 Breaking-News Latency Sample Effects]], Weak 2).

*(b) How do studies measure what is already priced in?* Five methods recur. (1) **Event-time returns split at publication**, with a placebo of comparable no-direction news: the placebo showed that much apparent post-news reversal was a background drift of covered small caps, and a sentiment-fade strategy (Sharpe 1.35) was identical to shorting every covered small cap ([[Kargarzadeh 2026 When Is News Priced In]]). Splitting first reports from follow-ups cut the measured earnings pre-drift from +1.41% to +1.02%, so follow-up coverage of moves already made inflates "anticipation" by about a third (same source). (2) **Pre-announcement drift windows** around scheduled releases: the pre-FOMC drift (49 bp in the 24 hours before announcements, 1994–2011, ≈80% of the equity premium, no reversal) ([[Lucca 2015 Pre-FOMC Announcement Drift]], Moderate 9), which then shrank to an insignificant ≈9 bp in 2016–2019 ([[Kurov 2021 Disappearing Pre-FOMC Drift]], Moderate 8); macro-futures drift in the 30 minutes before release ([[Kurov 2019 Price Drift Before Macro News]]). (3) **Option-market anticipation:** put-call ratios from buyer-initiated opening volume predicted next-day returns (≈42 bp/day low-minus-high quintile, >1% over a week, 1990–2001), decaying over about three weeks with no reversal, and the non-public open-buy signal subsumed the public one ([[Pan 2006 Option Volume Future Stock Prices]], Moderate 7); abnormal target-option volume (mainly short-dated OTM calls, 5–10 days before) in ≈25% of 1,859 takeovers, with public news explaining only ≈9% of flagged cases ([[Augustin 2019 Informed Options Trading Before Takeovers]], Moderate 9); in the cross-section, the IV spread and risk-neutral skewness predicted next-month returns in every regime 2015–2026 while the smirk faded after 2022, and option features forecast 5-day crashes in calm markets (AUC 0.71) but not when macro shocks dominated (0.56) ([[Li 2026 Option-Implied Signals Crash Risk]], Moderate 8). (4) **Jump tests on tick data** at the release time ([[Christensen 2026 Warp Speed Jumps After Earnings]]). (5) **Source and age attributes:** press wires show no pre-publication drift while commentary outlets show +0.8% to +1.1% (they write about moves already under way) ([[Kargarzadeh 2026 When Is News Priced In]]); signals from filings older than eight weeks added nothing net in a small RL test ([[Song 2026 When News Gets Old]]). *Overseas markets:* European indices also drifted before US FOMC announcements but not before their own central banks' ([[Lucca 2015 Pre-FOMC Announcement Drift]]). Lagged monthly US returns predict other industrialised markets out of sample (US Granger-causes 9 of 10; R²OS up to 3.8%) but not the reverse, read as US news reaching other markets with a lag ([[Rapach 2013 International Return Predictability US Role]], Moderate 9, read via abstract, digest and replication); an exact replication finds the gain is concentrated in US recessions (R²OS 4–13%) and absent in expansions ([[Siliverstovs 2015 US Role International Predictability Recessions]], Moderate 9).

*(c) What remains after publication?* Net of the placebo, quantified fundamental news (capital returns, earnings, guidance, analyst actions) keeps drifting for weeks (earnings +0.22% over days 6–20), soft story news (macro read-throughs, launches, leadership) reverses, and neutral news still widens price moves (guidance-day moves 1.29× normal) ([[Kargarzadeh 2026 When Is News Priced In]]). Such residuals are small next to costs and can decay after discovery ([[Kurov 2021 Disappearing Pre-FOMC Drift]]).

**Confidence:** High that scheduled numeric news in liquid US stocks and index futures is absorbed within milliseconds to minutes and that intraday trading on public releases earns nothing after realistic frictions (tick-level evidence with corrected tests; consistent with the near-zero post-publication drift in the large 2023–26 news study). Medium that a large part of the news-direction move happens before publication of the article a model would read (pre-release drift, options activity, commentary on moves in progress), and that options carry measurable anticipation (several Moderate studies). Medium that slower diffusion persists for news requiring linkage across firms, read mainly by retail investors, or about small, thinly covered firms (bad news slowest). Low on how fast information moves from social media to price at scale (channel latency is sample-dependent; no large market study opened). Low on overseas-market lead-lag as a priced-in measure: monthly US-to-foreign predictability exists out of sample but is regime-dependent and fragile; no intraday or same-event overseas study opened.

### RQ1.13 – Do autoregressive next-token models trained on market data (no LLM inside, e.g. Kronos, Chronos-style tokenized forecasters, decoder-only time-series models) beat continuous-input models and simple baselines? Do they improve with more data and model size, how fast do errors compound over multi-step rollouts, and how well do sampled token paths give calibrated probabilistic and tail forecasts?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-08 (10 new lit notes: 0 Strong, 8 Moderate, 2 Weak, in `01 Research/Lit Notes/price-time-series/` and `evaluation/`; plus [[Shi 2025 Kronos Financial Foundation Model]], [[Ansari 2024 Chronos Language of Time Series]], [[Mai 2024 StockGPT Generative Stock Returns]], [[Ma 2026 VAIOM Continuous-Input Discrete-Output]] and [[Ansari 2026 Chronos-2 Multivariate Foundation Model]] reused). Stop rule met at 15 sources (the guide's cap).

**Answer.** *Terms.* "Autoregressive next-token" covers two designs that the literature often merges: models that predict a categorical distribution over discrete tokens (Kronos, Chronos-T5, StockGPT, TradeFM, LOBS5) and decoder-only models that predict the next continuous patch (TimesFM, Timer). Both are autoregressive; only the first is tokenized. The evidence below is reported for each where it differs.

*(a) Do they beat continuous-input models and simple baselines?* Not under strong tests. The positive finance results come from finance-only pretraining and cross-sectional targets: Kronos beat generic TSFMs on IC/RankIC and GARCH on realised-volatility MAE in one post-2024 window without tests ([[Shi 2025 Kronos Financial Foundation Model]]); StockGPT's 50-bp return tokens gave a monthly long-short of ~13% a year with ~15% factor alpha, 2001–2023 ([[Mai 2024 StockGPT Generative Stock Returns]]); a continuous-input, bucket-output decoder beat a single-bar LightGBM by 0.02–0.04 bits/event on 2025 FX ([[Ma 2026 VAIOM Continuous-Input Discrete-Output]]). The best-designed per-series tests are negative. With expanding walk-forward folds, held-out tickers and an always-up base rate, LoRA-adapted and zero-shot TimesFM had no directional skill on S&P 500 or NASDAQ-100 at any horizon, and its MAE did not beat the naive forecast (S&P 15.15 vs 15.56) ([[Cheung 2026 Base-Rate-Honest TimesFM LoRA Equities]], Moderate 9). Financially fine-tuned TimesFM beat the original model but roughly tied AR(1) on S&P 500 (Sharpe 1.68 vs 1.58, cost-free) and lost to it on currencies (0.25 vs 0.88) ([[Fu 2024 Financial Fine-tuning TimesFM]]). Across 43 zero-shot TSFMs on 60K financial series since 2020, most did not beat persistence on relative MAE (best 0.981), no family won everywhere, and generic-benchmark rank predicted portfolio rank poorly (rank correlation 0.09) ([[Lee 2026 FinVerse Financial TSFM Benchmark]]). The token model Chronos was worse than a random walk on its one FX set ([[Ansari 2024 Chronos Language of Time Series]]), and its developers moved to continuous patches with a direct quantile head ([[Ansari 2026 Chronos-2 Multivariate Foundation Model]]). In microstructure, token-level generators beat classical generators on realism, not on forecasting: TradeFM had 2–3× lower return-distribution distance than a Compound Hawkes baseline ([[Kawawa-Beaudan 2026 TradeFM Trade-Flow Foundation Model]]), and the token-level LOBS5 beat a parametric model, a CGAN and BPE-tokenized RWKV models ([[Nagy 2025 LOB-Bench Generative LOB Benchmark]]).

*(b) Do they improve with more data and model size?* On smooth general data, yes: NLL follows power laws in parameters, compute and data in and out of distribution, encoder-only and decoder-only scale alike, but the token-based Chronos-T5 shows "very small" NLL exponents ([[Yao 2025 Neural Scaling Laws TSFMs]]). On market message streams, TradeFM's test loss follows L(C) ∝ C^−0.18 and L(D) ∝ D^−0.19, fitted from only three sizes on repeated data ([[Kawawa-Beaudan 2026 TradeFM Trade-Flow Foundation Model]]). For return-level targets, size saturates early: a 0.9M decoder beat 5M and 15M versions ([[Ma 2026 VAIOM Continuous-Input Discrete-Output]]); a decoder pretrained on chaotic series stopped improving beyond ~1M parameters, while the data needed to reach a fixed correlation grew roughly exponentially with horizon ([[Takemoto 2025 Chaotic Pretraining Scaling Bitcoin]], Weak); model size did not predict FinVerse rank ([[Lee 2026 FinVerse Financial TSFM Benchmark]]). A controlled study with known ground truth gives the mechanism: next-token training on simulated order flow reaches the Bayes-optimal predictor at ~10^5 parameters, and it is the **number of distinct regimes** in training, not token count, that governs generalisation (a one-regime model scored 3.57 nats on new regimes vs a 1.34 floor; convergence near 256 regimes) ([[Acero 2026 What LOB Foundation Models Learn]], Weak, mechanism only).

*(c) How fast do rollout errors compound?* Fast enough to matter. On fev-bench with a frozen Chronos-2, fully autoregressive patch-by-patch rollout was significantly worse than one-shot decoding (skill −3.11%; −10.73% and 0 of 19 wins on the longest horizons), finer chunks were worse than coarser ones, and a learned per-window schedule recovered only +0.2% to +0.7% ([[Kim 2026 Dynamic Multi-Step Decoding TSFMs]]). In generated order flow, distribution error rises with rollout step for every model, fastest for BPE-tokenized RWKV, whose impact curves diverged (ΔR 126 vs 2.45 for LOBS5) ([[Nagy 2025 LOB-Bench Generative LOB Benchmark]]). TradeFM's closed-loop rollouts kept stylized facts, but over only 1,024 events ([[Kawawa-Beaudan 2026 TradeFM Trade-Flow Foundation Model]]); simulated books stayed valid over thousands of steps but with a spread mismatch ([[Acero 2026 What LOB Foundation Models Learn]]). No study measured rollout error for daily or hourly price-token models.

*(d) Are sampled token paths calibrated?* The one systematic test says no, as implemented in Chronos. Across 14 TSFMs and 55 datasets, Chronos-T5 (autoregressive sampling over 4,096 bins) and the flow-matching Sundial were the worst calibrated (pooled error 0.102–0.147 vs 0.048–0.064 for parametric and quantile-head models), stayed off by more than ten times the nominal tail probability at the 1% quantile, and worsened with horizon (worst-step error 0.130 → 0.297 from short to long tasks); post-hoc recalibration fixed average coverage more than conditional errors ([[Decker 2026 Calibration of Probabilistic TSFMs]]). On equities, TimesFM's quantile head was also miscalibrated in the tail (nominal 5% quantile covered ~44%) ([[Cheung 2026 Base-Rate-Honest TimesFM LoRA Equities]]). Generated microstructure paths do reproduce heavy tails, volatility clustering and aggregational Gaussianity ([[Kawawa-Beaudan 2026 TradeFM Trade-Flow Foundation Model]]), which is unconditional realism, not conditional calibration. No finance study compares VaR/ES from sampled token paths with GARCH/HAR or conditional EVT (RQ1.6).

**Confidence:** Low that autoregressive next-token market models beat strong baselines (random walk/AR, GARCH/HAR, GBT) for per-series returns or direction; the best-designed tests are negative and the positive results are cross-sectional, single-window or untested. Medium that finance-specific pretraining matters more than architecture (consistent with RQ1.1). Medium that, for return-level targets, model size saturates early and regime diversity of the training data matters more than volume (one synthetic mechanism study, one weak and two finance studies agree; microstructure loss still scales). Medium that fully autoregressive rollout compounds error and that direct multi-step output is the safer default for point forecasts (general and LOB data agree). Medium that Chronos-style sampled token paths are poorly calibrated without recalibration; Low for finance specifically.

Basis: the lit notes above (full-text excerpts). *Kronos* (finance-only pretraining on K-lines; target cross-sectional IC and volatility MAE; one window, no tests) and *StockGPT* (finance-only, return tokens; cross-sectional monthly portfolios, 23 years, factor alphas) are positive. *Cheung* (generic TimesFM, LoRA; per-series direction and MAE; walk-forward with FDR-corrected tests), *Fu* (TimesFM continually pretrained on finance; per-series direction and market-neutral PnL; one split) and *FinVerse* (43 generic TSFMs zero-shot; point, IC and portfolio metrics) are negative or mixed. ### RQ1.14 – In what form should news, social posts and events enter a non-LLM autoregressive market model: sentiment or event tokens from a small encoder (such as FinBERT), structured event codes (such as GDELT/CAMEO), learned text embeddings, or text tokens interleaved with market tokens? Which forms carry signal, at what cost, and which avoid look-ahead and memorisation?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-08 (9 new lit notes: 1 Strong, 2 Moderate, 6 Weak, in `01 Research/Lit Notes/sentiment/` and `fusion/`; plus [[Foley 2026 NoLBERT News Topics Returns]], [[Yan 2026 DatedGPT Time-Aware Pretraining]], [[Li 2025 Structured Event Representation Returns]], [[Guo 2025 Factors and Newsflow Fusion Returns]], [[Sridhar 2026 Text Sensitivity Audit Multimodal TS]], [[Koval 2025 Modality-Specific Experts Text and TS]], [[Montgomery 2026 Numeric Value Encoding Transformers]], [[Kirtac 2024 Sentiment Trading with LLMs]] and [[Lopez-Lira 2025 LLM Memorization Problem]] reused). Stop rule met at the 15-source cap (18 tagged); the last three new sources added one new finding between them (occurred vs forward-looking news).

**Answer.** *Terms.* Five forms were found: (1) a scalar or 3-way sentiment score from a small encoder (FinBERT-class, ~110M parameters); (2) a small set of descriptors beyond polarity (dispersion across articles, intensity, uncertainty, news volume, event type, horizon), usually extracted by an LLM; (3) structured event codes (GDELT EventCode/Goldstein, subject–action–object triplets); (4) dense embeddings from a frozen or tuned encoder, pooled per day or per firm; (5) text tokens interleaved with market tokens in one sequence. No study compared all five on the same data, and none used a non-LLM autoregressive market model as the host, so the answer is assembled from partial comparisons.

*(a) Which forms carry signal?* **Scalar sentiment adds little to strong price baselines for per-series targets.** Market-wide ModernFinBERT variables beat HAR in only 2 of 33 cells and neither win survived longer HAC bandwidths; a naive forecast had the lowest mean QLIKE ([[Lis 2026 ModernFinBERT News Volatility HAR]], Moderate 9). Point-in-time NoLBERT topics did not beat a price-only benchmark net of costs ([[Foley 2026 NoLBERT News Topics Returns]], Strong 10). A FinBERT score inside a price Transformer left direction at ~50% ([[Jeong 2026 FinBERT Embeddings vs Scalar Sentiment]], Weak). **The positive evidence is cross-sectional and uses encoders fine-tuned on return labels:** with a post-cutoff test (Jun 2024–Jan 2026), 5 bp costs and a 10% ADV cap, a fine-tuned FinBERT long–short earned net Sharpe 1.75, RoBERTa 2.25 and LLaMA-3-8B 2.85, against 0.68 for the Loughran–McDonald dictionary, with SPA and deflated-Sharpe tests passed ([[Kirtac 2026 MFAST News Trading Under Frictions]], Strong 10, absolute numbers flagged as outliers); the earlier, random-split version of the same comparison gave the same ranking ([[Kirtac 2024 Sentiment Trading with LLMs]]). **Descriptors beyond polarity carry part of the signal.** Across studies the most important features were cross-article dispersion and volume-weighted tone ([[Zhang 2025 GDELT FinBERT Macro Alpha]]; [[Dai 2026 Multi-Dimensional Sentiment WTI]], where FinBERT polarity dispersion ranked second), event type and horizon fields (+0.019 F1 over FinBERT, but AUROC only 0.526 → 0.528; [[Zhu 2026 Beyond Sentiment Structured News Extraction]]), and whether the news reports an event that has happened or an expectation (Sharpe 0.62 vs −0.01 on aluminium; [[Paredes Amorin 2026 Conditional Sentiment Aluminum]]). Article counts alone added little once tone was included ([[Dai 2026 Multi-Dimensional Sentiment WTI]]). All of these are Weak except Zhang (Moderate by rubric, results implausible). **Structured event codes:** LLM-extracted event triplets beat sentiment and FinBERT embeddings for daily cross-sectional returns ([[Li 2025 Structured Event Representation Returns]], capped Weak); GDELT EventCode filtering plus Goldstein scores appear in one macro study whose Sharpe ratios of 4.6–5.9 are not credible ([[Zhang 2025 GDELT FinBERT Macro Alpha]]). Evidence on GDELT/CAMEO codes as model inputs is therefore **thin** (fewer than three usable studies). **Dense embeddings:** daily mean-pooled frozen FinBERT embeddings beat a scalar FinBERT score by 17% MSE inside a price Transformer, and attention pooling over articles did worse than mean pooling ([[Jeong 2026 FinBERT Embeddings vs Scalar Sentiment]]); bottlenecked DeBERTa embeddings concatenated with ~200 factors helped only where news was complementary and news alone was useless ([[Guo 2025 Factors and Newsflow Fusion Returns]]). **Interleaved tokens:** tested only with actual LLM backbones. A frozen Qwen3-8B with price tokens plus a news-reading LoRA reviser cut daily SP500 MAPE at H = 64 from 15.3% (price tokens only) to 9.8%, but the gain mixes news with SFT and RL revision ([[Zhao 2026 DualCast Dual-Path Bimodal Forecasting]], Weak); separating time-series experts from text layers mattered more than fusion depth in a Llama3-8B ([[Koval 2025 Modality-Specific Experts Text and TS]], Weak). No study of interleaved text tokens in a non-LLM market model was found. **Placebo controls matter.** Shuffling text across timestamps changed MSE by ≤1% on Economy and Energy series ([[Liang 2026 TAC-Time Texts as Channels]]), and replacing text with empty or shuffled input moved MSE under 0.5% in three published multimodal models ([[Sridhar 2026 Text Sensitivity Audit Multimodal TS]]); a text-on vs text-off ablation does not show that text content is used.

*(b) At what cost?* Per article, fine-tuned FinBERT used 4.4 GB and 8.5 ms (118 articles/s) against 54.2 GB and 112 ms (9 articles/s) for LLaMA-3-8B ([[Kirtac 2026 MFAST News Trading Under Frictions]]). Unfreezing even one FinBERT layer inside a forecaster raised trainable parameters from 75k to 7.7M and was abandoned ([[Jeong 2026 FinBERT Embeddings vs Scalar Sentiment]]). Structured extraction with a 70B model needs one pass per article but is then reusable across models and horizons ([[Zhu 2026 Beyond Sentiment Structured News Extraction]]). The interleaved LLM reviser cost ~16× the fast path's latency (5.85 s vs 0.37 s per window) ([[Zhao 2026 DualCast Dual-Path Bimodal Forecasting]]). A GPT-2-based text-channel model needed 1.9 GB and 13M trainable parameters ([[Liang 2026 TAC-Time Texts as Channels]]).

*(c) Which forms avoid look-ahead and memorisation?* The risk sits in the encoder or extractor, not in the form. Five of the nine new studies ran an LLM (LLaMA-3.1-70B, GPT-4o, Qwen3, Llama-3.2) on news inside its training window without a control and were capped Weak; LLM recall of pre-cutoff market facts survives masking instructions ([[Lopez-Lira 2025 LLM Memorization Problem]]). Fixed small encoders reduce but do not remove the risk: ModernFinBERT's training cutoff is undocumented, so even a careful study treats it as an ex-post instrument ([[Lis 2026 ModernFinBERT News Volatility HAR]]). Two clean options exist: test only after the encoder's documented cutoff ([[Kirtac 2026 MFAST News Trading Under Frictions]]) or use dated encoders pretrained on text before each test window, which still find signal ([[Foley 2026 NoLBERT News Topics Returns]]; [[Yan 2026 DatedGPT Time-Aware Pretraining]]). Structured fields extracted by an LLM inherit its cutoff problem; GDELT records are machine-coded at ingestion time (point-in-time), but headlines re-fetched later from GDELT URLs are not ([[Zhang 2025 GDELT FinBERT Macro Alpha]]).

**Confidence:** Medium that a scalar small-encoder sentiment score adds little beyond strong price baselines for per-series return and volatility targets (one Strong and one Moderate study agree; weak studies consistent). Medium that encoders fine-tuned on return labels give tradable cross-sectional signal after costs, with larger models better but ~13× slower (one Strong post-cutoff study plus an earlier weak one; absolute size unverified). Low that descriptors beyond polarity (dispersion, intensity, event type, occurred vs forward-looking) are the better compact form; all evidence is Weak but consistent across four studies. Low on dense embeddings vs codes vs tokens. Thin evidence on GDELT/CAMEO codes and on interleaved tokens in non-LLM models. Medium that point-in-time control depends on the encoder's documented cutoff or on dated encoders, whatever the form.

### RQ1.15 – World-model hypothesis: does conditioning a market sequence model on a broader state of the world (macro, cross-asset, company graph, events) make forecasts more accurate than a price-only model, for which targets and horizons, and when does the added state hurt? What do financial and general world-model studies report?

**Status:** answered 2026-11-08 (9 new lit notes: 1 Strong, 3 Moderate, 5 Weak, in `01 Research/Lit Notes/price-time-series/`, `fusion/`, `graph/` and `evaluation/`, tag `world-model`; plus [[Ye 2026 Regime-Gated Residual MoE Volatility]], [[Wade 2026 GNN Volatility Forecasts Portfolios]], [[Pu 2023 Network Momentum Across Asset Classes]], [[Guo 2025 Factors and Newsflow Fusion Returns]], [[Zhang 2025 When Multimodality Helps TS Forecasting]] and [[Ansari 2026 Chronos-2 Multivariate Foundation Model]] reused). Stop rule met at the 15-source cap; the last three new sources ([[Hu 2026 Exogenous Dropout Corruption-Robust Covariates]], [[Bai 2026 Covariate TSFMs Forecast Associations Not Interventions]], [[Chen 2024 Deep Learning in Asset Pricing]]) added one new finding (what-if answers are associational) and confirmed two.

**Answer.** *Terms.* "World state" here means inputs beyond the target asset's own price and volume history: macro series, other assets (cross-asset), a company or market graph, and events or text. No finance study tested a full world-state model against a price-only one on the same data. The answer is assembled from studies that add one kind of state at a time.

*(a) Does added state beat price-only?* **Only modestly, and only when the state is compressed and its influence is structured.** *Macro:* feeding monthly point-in-time macro factors through regime-retrieval attention lifted gross time-series-momentum Sharpe on 55 futures from 1.19 (macro-free LSTM) to 1.99, but concatenating the same five macro PCs to the daily inputs collapsed it to 0.06, and shuffling the macro history cut it to 0.81 ([[Oliveira 2026 HANET Macro-Aware Mixed-Frequency Attention]], Moderate 8). In a no-arbitrage asset-pricing network, 178 raw macro series made out-of-sample Sharpe collapse for every model, while four LSTM macro states added only about 10% ([[Chen 2024 Deep Learning in Asset Pricing]]). For cross-sectional volatility of 1,027 US stocks, regime variables appended as inputs lowered IC (−0.0023 for ridge) and made MLPs collapse in 30 of 30 seeds; routed through a gate they added +0.0004 ([[Ye 2026 Regime-Gated Residual MoE Volatility]], Strong 11). Simple macro features (VIX, credit and term spreads) did improve every graph model for weekly S&P 500 volatility (best MSE 0.0298 vs HAR 0.0329) ([[Wade 2026 GNN Volatility Forecasts Portfolios]]). *Cross-asset:* within one asset class, related series helped a foundation model (Treasury MAPE 0.050 vs 0.123 univariate), but adding the other class (stocks with rates) slightly hurt ([[Das 2026 Chronos-2 Multivariate Financial Forecasting]], Weak). For eight equity indices, foreign-market information cut volatility MSE by only 1.70% at 1 day and 1.13% at 22 days, and only as a bounded correction to each market's own HAR; unrestricted foreign regressors (VHAR) raised 22-day MSE by 33% and unanchored graph and attention models were worse than HAR at every lead ([[Zhao 2026 PGA-Trans-HAR Cross-Market Volatility]], Moderate 9). Cross-asset network momentum beat own momentum gross but not beyond about 3 bps per trade ([[Pu 2023 Network Momentum Across Asset Classes]]). *Graph and events:* see RQ1.4 (graph value small and edge-type dependent) and RQ1.14 and RQ1.7 (text adds little to strong price models; multimodal models did not beat the best unimodal model on average, [[Zhang 2025 When Multimodality Helps TS Forecasting]]). *Market state itself:* among 18 encoders trained as world-model state layers on 1 Hz data for all US stocks (2008–2025), a supervised multihead encoder was best (15-minute rank IC 0.031 for returns, 0.094 for volatility change), above Chronos-2, TimesFM 3.0 and Kronos; most self-supervised objectives barely beat an untrained random encoder, and cross-stock views that capture market-wide state did not raise predictive IC ([[Merchant 2026 Towards Financial World Modeling]], Strong 12).

*(b) For which targets and horizons?* Volatility gains show up at short and long leads but not the middle: the cross-market correction helped at 1 and 22 days and was level with HAR at 5 days, with gains differing by market ([[Zhao 2026 PGA-Trans-HAR Cross-Market Volatility]]). Macro conditioning helped slow-horizon timing decisions (monthly regimes conditioning daily positions; monthly SDF) ([[Oliveira 2026 HANET Macro-Aware Mixed-Frequency Attention]]; [[Chen 2024 Deep Learning in Asset Pricing]]), but high-turnover gains vanished with costs: HANET momentum net Sharpe was 1.24 at 2 bps and 0.17 at 5 bps, while its slower carry strategy kept 0.60 at 10 bps. At intraday horizons only per-stock market state was tested, and skill varied far more across months than across methods (month explained 97–99.9% of variance) ([[Merchant 2026 Towards Financial World Modeling]]). Evidence on returns at daily horizons is weakest. Lagged cross-market returns follow the same pattern: lagged US returns improve monthly forecasts of other markets out of sample, but the gain sits in US recessions and is absent in expansions ([[Rapach 2013 International Return Predictability US Role]]; [[Siliverstovs 2015 US Role International Predictability Recessions]]).

*(c) When does added state hurt?* Six conditions recur. (1) **Naive concatenation** of many or low-frequency variables (HANET LSTM + macro PCs; raw macro in [[Chen 2024 Deep Learning in Asset Pricing]]; regime inputs in [[Ye 2026 Regime-Gated Residual MoE Volatility]]; VHAR). (2) **Unrelated context**: cross-class series ([[Das 2026 Chronos-2 Multivariate Financial Forecasting]]); news in emerging markets ([[Guo 2025 Factors and Newsflow Fusion Returns]]). (3) **A strong local model already captures the signal**: multimodal gains correlated 0.90 with the weakness of the unimodal model ([[Zhang 2025 When Multimodality Helps TS Forecasting]]); no gain over HAR at 5 days. (4) **Corrupted feeds in deployment**: covariate models that beat the price-only floor by 18–28% on clean electricity-price covariates were 19–34% worse than the floor when the covariates were noised, shifted or missing; dropping whole covariate channels during training largely fixed this ([[Hu 2026 Exogenous Dropout Corruption-Robust Covariates]], Moderate 7). (5) **Costs** on faster signals (above). (6) **Over-reading events**: assuming a storm's effect would persist raised one forecast's error by 93% ([[Liu 2026 World2TS Real-World Event Context]], Weak).

*(d) What do general world-model studies report?* Predicting the encoded future state (JEPA style) from history plus covariates, then decoding, beat observation-space training and covariate-free variants on 21 non-financial datasets ([[Zhu 2026 WorldTS Latent World Modeling Covariates]], Weak); finance-specific state layers are only starting to be benchmarked ([[Merchant 2026 Towards Financial World Modeling]]). Two cautions carry over. Event context linked to series by hindsight improved LLM forecasters in 49 of 54 comparisons, but the events were chosen because they explained the series and the LLMs read pre-cutoff data ([[Liu 2026 World2TS Real-World Event Context]]). And a model trained on observational data answers "what if" queries with associations: four covariate-aware foundation models over-stated confounded effects by 1.3–1.8× relative to least squares and reported effects of 0.44–0.72 where the true effect was zero ([[Bai 2026 Covariate TSFMs Forecast Associations Not Interventions]], Weak, synthetic). A market world model used for scenario analysis would need interventional training or experimental data for that use.

**Confidence:** Medium that compressed, point-in-time macro or cross-asset state used through a structured pathway (regime attention, gating, a bounded correction to a strong local model) gives modest gains over price-only for volatility and slow-horizon allocation, while naive concatenation hurts (one Strong and several Moderate studies agree). Low on how large the gain is for returns after costs. Medium that added state hurts when it is unrelated, redundant with a strong local model, or corrupted at deployment. Thin evidence on a combined multi-source world-state model against price-only in finance (none found). Low that observationally trained world models give causal what-if answers (one synthetic, non-financial study, but with a formal argument).

1. **Strong simple baselines decide the verdict.** Gains shrink or vanish as study quality rises. Random walk and GBT for returns, HAR for volatility, AR(1) and buy-and-hold or 1/N for trading rules beat or match most complex models in the best-designed tests (RQ1.1, RQ1.7, RQ1.9, RQ1.13; e.g. [[Gu 2020 Empirical Asset Pricing Machine Learning]], [[Saly-Kaufmann 2026 Deep Learning Financial Benchmark]], [[Pollok 2026 End-to-End Policies vs Simple Rules]], [[Cheung 2026 Base-Rate-Honest TimesFM LoRA Equities]]). Positive results cluster in studies with weak baselines, one test window or no costs.
2. **Risk is forecastable; direction barely is.** Volatility and tail models improve with regime and cross-market information, while return forecasts stay weak and state-dependent; the more reliable ML gains in the sources read are in risk forecasts, not return direction (RQ1.1, RQ1.6, RQ1.15; [[Fang 2026 Volatility Regimes Return Prediction China]], [[Christensen 2023 Machine Learning Volatility Forecasting]]).
3. **Structure beats capacity when inputs are added.** Text, macro, cross-asset and graph inputs help only through compressed, gated or bounded pathways that keep a strong price model intact; naive concatenation and unconstrained attention hurt, and corrupted feeds make models worse than price-only (RQ1.7, RQ1.14, RQ1.15; [[Ye 2026 Regime-Gated Residual MoE Volatility]], [[Oliveira 2026 HANET Macro-Aware Mixed-Frequency Attention]], [[Hu 2026 Exogenous Dropout Corruption-Robust Covariates]]).
4. **Point-in-time integrity dominates the text evidence.** Pre-cutoff LLM results are inflated by memorisation, and the cleanest designs (post-cutoff data, dated encoders, blinded tests) give much smaller or null effects (RQ1.2, RQ1.3, RQ1.14; [[Lopez-Lira 2025 LLM Memorization Problem]], [[Glasserman 2023 Look-Ahead Bias GPT Sentiment]], [[Yan 2026 DatedGPT Time-Aware Pretraining]]). The rubric cap for uncontrolled LLM use applied to many 2025–26 papers.
5. **Results are regime-dependent, in both directions.** Some gains appear only in stress (regime-aware volatility forecasts; US-to-foreign lead-lag in recessions), others only in calm markets (return direction; option-implied crash signals), and no tail method is calibrated at shock onset (RQ1.6, RQ1.8, RQ1.12, RQ1.15; [[Siliverstovs 2015 US Role International Predictability Recessions]], [[Li 2026 Option-Implied Signals Crash Risk]]). Pooled averages hide this.
6. **Speed sets the usable horizon.** Public, scheduled, numeric news in liquid assets is priced in milliseconds to seconds, and much of a news move precedes the article. The slower, plausibly exploitable channels are cross-firm links, small and thinly covered stocks, repeated news and cross-market monthly lead-lag (RQ1.4, RQ1.11, RQ1.12; [[Chordia 2018 Low-Latency Trading Macro Announcements]], [[Cohen 2008 Economic Links Predictable Returns]], [[Kargarzadeh 2026 When Is News Priced In]]).
7. **Detection and conditional risk, not prediction of timing.** No source predicts the timing of exogenous shocks; what holds up is rising probability from build-ups over months to years and faster detection after onset (RQ1.5, RQ1.8; [[Greenwood 2022 Predictable Financial Crises]], [[Guttal 2016 No Critical Slowing Down Before Crashes]]).
8. **The token-model premise is untested where it matters.** No matched finance test compares tokenized and continuous models with the same backbone against GARCH/HAR and GBT under walk-forward tests and costs; scaling saturates early on return targets; rollouts compound error (RQ1.10, RQ1.13).
9. **Evidence quality is thin at the frontier.** Only 14 of 163 sources score Strong. Most 2025–26 arXiv studies are Weak, with single windows, no costs or uncontrolled LLMs, and a few report outlier effects (Sharpe ≈ 6, 79% direction accuracy) that no other study comes close to. Older journal studies are stronger but often read here only as working papers or summaries.

## Open questions for the methodology plan

- Should trading decisions be made by a forecast-then-decide pipeline (forecaster + transparent allocation or MPC-style planner) rather than an end-to-end RL agent, given that RL agents did not beat 1/N or buy-and-hold in the strongest tests? (from RQ1.9; also RQ4.6)
- If any RL or learned policy is tried, should acceptance require beating 1/N, risk parity and TSMOM net of costs, averaged over seeds, and passing a probability-of-backtest-overfitting check? (from RQ1.9; also RQ4.1)
- Should the default adaptation be plain rolling retraining (or a batch-norm statistics refresh), with MAML/Reptile, continual-learning tools or regime libraries accepted only if they beat it in a walk-forward test? (from RQ1.9; also RQ4.9)
- Is there any role for tree search (MCTS) or A\*-style planning, given evidence only for non-convex execution/hedging problems with a known dynamics model? (from RQ1.9; bears on the archived A\*/TD3/MAML prototype, reviewed in RP5)

- Should the project aim to *detect* shocks fast and estimate their spread, plus a slow fragility gauge (credit/price booms, news novelty) for raised probability over months, rather than to *predict* the timing of shocks? (from RQ1.8; also RQ1.5)
- Should crisis or shock classifiers be restricted to simple, regularised models (logit, small GBT) given tens of events, with flexible models accepted only if they beat them in recursive tests? (from RQ1.8; also RQ4.4)
- Should every shock model be tested on held-out extremes larger than any in training (a gray-swan test), and should "unprecedented" be judged against all analogous events pooled across markets and eras? (from RQ1.8; also RQ4.5)
- Which alarm metric: a fixed, pre-registered false-alarm/miss cost ratio with a proper scoring rule, rather than F1, MCC or AUC alone? (from RQ1.8; also RQ4.3)

- Should text enter only through a gate, residual adapter or mixture expert on top of a price/factor model that works on its own, never by plain concatenation into the main model? (from RQ1.7; also RQ4.4)
- Should every multimodal claim be tested with shuffled and placebo text, and with numeric side channels removed? (from RQ1.7; also RQ4.5)
- Should a cheap information test (mutual information or permutation test of text against residual returns) be run before any fusion model is built? (from RQ1.7)
- Which baseline must fusion beat: GBT on factors and price features, not a deep price-only model? (from RQ1.7; also RQ1.1)
- Should regime or state variables enter only via routing (soft gates) rather than as inputs? (from RQ1.7; also RQ1.6, RQ4.9)

- Should every tail output sit on a volatility-filtered, fat-tailed base (GARCH-t/skew-t or conditional EVT), with any ML or foundation model judged against it and a conformal layer added on top? (from RQ1.6; also RQ4.3)
- Which conformal scheme, if any: recency-weighted calibration (better in stress) or feedback methods such as ACI (better long-run average)? (from RQ1.6)
- Should tail models pool across many assets (cross-sectional training) rather than fit per asset, and at what horizon? (from RQ1.6; also RQ1.1)
- How should tail forecasts be evaluated around crisis onsets: regime-stratified exceedances, first-20-day coverage, DQ tests? (from RQ1.6; also RQ4.3, RQ4.5)

- Should shock detection be split into a slow risk-state gauge (news novelty, monthly) and a fast event detector (bursts filtered by a learned market-impact classifier), evaluated separately? (from RQ1.5; also RQ4.3, RQ4.5)
- Should event detectors use unsupervised anomaly scores with fixed equal source weights rather than learned weights, given very few positive labels? (from RQ1.5; also RQ4.4)
- Which baseline must a text-based warning beat: VIX and realised-volatility persistence at the same horizon? (from RQ1.5; also RQ4.3)
- Should the system aim to detect shocks and then propagate them along supply-chain links, rather than predict them? (from RQ1.5, RQ1.4)

- Should graph information enter first as simple neighbour-average features in a GBT, with a GNN only if it beats that on the same graph? (from RQ1.4)
- Which graph sources are worth building point-in-time: analyst co-coverage (licensed IBES), filings-based supply chain, rolling correlation, LLM-extracted relations? (from RQ1.4; also RQ2.8, RQ3.7)
- How should message passing handle crisis periods when correlation graphs become nearly complete? (from RQ1.4; also RQ1.6)

- What forecast horizon and rebalancing frequency keep turnover low enough that the signals survive costs? (from RQ1.1)
- Should finance-native pretraining be attempted at all, given its compute cost, versus GBT plus HAR baselines? (from RQ1.1)
- Should forecasts or exposure be conditioned on a volatility regime? (from RQ1.1; also RQ1.6)
- If LLMs read text, which kind: point-in-time models (NoLBERT, DatedGPT, PIT) for backtests, and frontier APIs only on live or post-release data? (from RQ1.2)
- At what time is news turned into a position, given that pre-open news is largely priced at the open? (from RQ1.2; also RQ3.2)
- Should the build use an LLM backbone for numeric series at all, given the null results? (from RQ1.2)
- Should sentiment tools be chosen by return association on held-out, time-ordered data rather than by label accuracy? (from RQ1.3; bears on the current FinBERT/nlptown/VADER module, reviewed in RP5)
- Should text signals be aggregated over a week rather than a day, given the longer predictability of weekly tone? (from RQ1.3)
- Should social-media attention (volume) and sentiment enter as separate features, with spam and bot filtering first? (from RQ1.3; also RQ3.2)
- Should the model predict a categorical distribution over volatility-normalised return buckets (with an ordinal or distance-aware loss), while feeding market inputs as continuous or hybrid-binned vectors rather than tokens? (from RQ1.10)
- If tokens are used, what scaling, range and vocabulary size, and how are tails beyond the bin range handled (clipping, tail buckets, separate magnitude channel)? (from RQ1.10)
- Should the first build include a matched ablation (same backbone and output; tokenized vs continuous vs hybrid input) as an explicit experiment? (from RQ1.10; also RQ4.4)
- Should the model emit the whole horizon directly (multi-step head) rather than roll out token by token, and if rollouts are needed (scenario generation), how is drift checked against real data? (from RQ1.13)
- Should training data be chosen for regime diversity (many markets, periods, asset classes, possibly simulated regimes) rather than raw volume, and how is that measured? (from RQ1.13; also RQ4.8)
- Should probabilistic outputs be recalibrated on past windows (and checked in the tails with Kupiec/Christoffersen tests) before any risk use? (from RQ1.13; also RQ1.6, RQ4.3)
- Should the first benchmark include an always-up/base-rate baseline, AR(1), HAR/GARCH and GBT next to any foundation model, scored separately per series and cross-sectionally? (from RQ1.13; also RQ4.2)
- Which compact text form should the first model use: a return-supervised small-encoder score per firm-day plus a few descriptors (dispersion, volume, event type, occurred vs forward-looking), with dense embeddings and event codes as later ablations? (from RQ1.14; also RQ1.7)
- Should every text-input ablation include placebo text (shuffled across dates, constant, empty) as well as text-off, so that gains from routing or extra numeric inputs are not credited to text? (from RQ1.14; also RQ4.4)
- Which encoder satisfies point-in-time control: one with a documented training cutoff before the test window, or a family of dated encoders (NoLBERT/DatedGPT-style) refreshed per window? (from RQ1.14; also RQ3.1, RQ4.5)

- Should added world state (macro, cross-asset, graph, events) enter only through structured, limited pathways (regime attention, gating, a bounded correction to a strong price-only or HAR-style model), with plain concatenation used only as a negative control? (from RQ1.15; also RQ1.7)
- Should every added-state ablation include a corrupted-feed test (noise, delay, missing source) and train with whole-source dropout, so that the model degrades to the price-only floor rather than below it? (from RQ1.15; also RQ4.4)
- Should scenario ("what if") use of the model be out of scope unless it is trained or validated on interventional or natural-experiment data? (from RQ1.15; also RQ4.5)
- Should the text pipeline store, per item, a point-in-time repetition score (overlap with earlier items on the same entity), a first-report flag and a source identifier, so that repetition, recombination and source reliability can be tested as features or filters rather than assumed? (from RQ1.11; also RQ3.2)
- Should provenance (which feed, which original reporter) be taken only from data the pipeline controls, since publisher labels in crawled corpora can be forged? (from RQ1.11; also RQ2.2)
- Should every news-derived feature carry first-report vs follow-up status, source type (wire vs commentary) and time since first trace, and be benchmarked against a no-direction (neutral-coverage) placebo before any gain is credited to text? (from RQ1.12 and RQ1.11; also RQ4.5)
- Should option-implied signals (IV spread, risk-neutral skew, abnormal OTM call volume) be part of the "world state", given that options often move before public news? (from RQ1.12; also RQ2.1)
